# Chess scene and UI (src/Chess/Components, config/SceneConfig.xml)

Part of the Chess documentation (a section named in quotes may be in a sibling file: the opponent in `src/Chess/Engine/CLAUDE.md`, the rules in `src/Chess/Game/CLAUDE.md`, the tests in `tests/CLAUDE.md`). **Read this before changing `config/SceneConfig.xml`**: the HUD, the panels, the coordinates and the captured material are described here.

## Files

```
└── Components/               scene glue (IComponent)
    ├── ChessBoardComponent   the board: draws squares and highlights, turns clicks and drags into OnSquareClicked, runs the opponent's and the analyst's Update() on every SceneUpdateEvent, publishes the UIVars of the HUD and the panels, answers the UI actions
    ├── ChessMaterialComponent  on the node `Material`, before its canvas: the sprites of the ten icons, the twelve `material...` variables, the rows shown or hidden
    ├── ChessPieceComponent   logical handle of one piece; drives its TransformComponent from the model (a played move is slid) and switches its sprite after a promotion
    └── ChessResultComponent  on its own node (layer 1, over the pieces): dims the board and draws the result bar once the game is over
```

`ChessBoardComponent` ignores a click while a program plays the side to move (either seat); the Reset button, a click after the end (it starts a new game) and "Two players" in the panel work as always. In a build without Stockfish (`CHESS_NO_STOCKFISH`) it hides the segment `EngineStockfish` once, at its first update or draw (`HideUnavailableEngines`).

## How it works

- A piece node is a `ChessPieceComponent` + `SpriteRendererComponent` (artwork from `config/TextureLibrary.xml`) + `TransformComponent`. `OnAttached` registers the piece with the model (a piece that registers late is dealt to the position as it stands); the model moves a handle with its piece through captures, castling, en passant and promotion (the sprite switches to `<Color><Type>` when the slide is over).
- **A played move slides**; the animation is the component's only, the model, the control API and the version know nothing of it. A change is a **move** only when the count of moves and the version are each exactly one more, the start position is the same, the frame has time in it (`deltaTime > 0`), `_slideDuration > 0` and the piece was seen before; a `ScopedTween` then runs a progress 0..1 and the position is the `Lerp` of the pixels of the two squares taken from `SquareToPixel` on every update. **Everything else is a snap** (the first sighting, `Reset()`, `set_state`, several moves between two frames, a falling count), and a snap ends a slide under way. **A frame with no time snaps** (a bare `UpdateAll()`, a `screenshot` frame of the manual clock, `step` with `dt` 0), so the UI tests need no waiting.  A captured piece stays for the same time and is then moved off-screen (no fade); a dropped piece snaps (see "The drag"). `_slideDuration` is a reflected field of each piece node, not set in `SceneConfig.xml`. Held by `ChessPieceAnimationTest`.
- **The node is the whole square, the sprite is fitted inside it.** The node is `squareSize` wide and high, and the sprite of each of the 32 piece nodes is `fit="Contain"` (`engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`): as high as the square, centered, with the aspect of its tall atlas cell. A new piece node needs the attribute (`Stretch`, the default, squashes the cell). The atlas `assets/chess_test.png` must keep a real alpha channel.
- **Board geometry** (`_originX`, `_originY`, `_squareSize`) is reflected, in scene units, not pixels: `ChessScene` declares `viewWidth="840" viewHeight="800"` (the board 640 x 640 at (100, 80)) and the engine scales the scene to the window. Change the view size together with the board geometry; map the mouse with `Scene::ScreenToSceneX/Y`. **The board is drawn from Black's side exactly when the program plays White** (`BottomColor(OpponentController::GetPlays())`): the grid turns by 180 degrees and the person's pieces are at the bottom; two people and the program as Black keep White there. `GetBottomColor()` reads the opponent **live on every use** (no cached flag, so it follows the panel and `set_state`), `SquareToPixel` maps a square to its cell and `OnMouseDown` maps the cell back (`Game/BoardOrientation.h`). The dim, the HUD and the panels do not turn; neither do the model, the control API (`get_state.board` stays rank 8 first), UCI and FEN.
- **The coordinates** are 16 labels (`RankLabel0..7`, `FileLabel0..7`) of a `SceneView` canvas on the node `BoardCoordinates` (`layer="-1"`, after `Board`; `useSafeArea="false"`, because an inset must not move a label; no `blocksInput`). **The rects are fixed and the text turns:** each label shows `{coordRank<row>}` / `{coordFile<column>}`, `UIVars` that `OnDraw()` publishes from `Game/BoardCoordinates.h` for the `bottom` color, before the model check and only when it changed. **The contrast is static:** the turn keeps the parity of the grid (a1 is dark in both orientations), so a label has the dark brown on a light cell and the cream on a dark one, and `IsLightSquare` is the one function the board paints and the tests check with. Held by `ChessUiTest`.
- **The hint on the board.** While the analyst holds a hint for the position on the board (`PositionAnalyst::GetHint(model)`) and the game is not over, `OnDraw()` fills its two squares (`kHintFromSquare`, `kHintToSquare`) with the same `SquareToPixel(..., bottom, ...)` as every highlight, so they follow the turned board. **Draw order inside the board:** squares, the last move, the check, the hint, then the selection and the legal moves (so a selection stays readable), then the coordinates (layer -1) and the pieces (layer 0). The hint belongs to the model's version: a move (by anyone), a reset, a `set_state` or a `SetPosition` take it off at the next frame; a click that only selects keeps it; while the engine thinks or has failed nothing is drawn.
- **The last move on the board.** `OnDraw()` fills the from and the to square of `GetMoves().back()` in `kLastMoveSquare`, right after the checkered base. The component keeps nothing: the mark is read from the model on every draw, so a move by anyone shows it, also the move that ends the game (under the dim). Castling marks the king's squares only, en passant the pawn's from and to, a pending promotion nothing; a reset, `SetPosition` and `set_state` with a FEN clear `GetMoves()`.
- **The captured material** (what each side has taken, and who is ahead) sits in the **left** margin of the view by the node `Material`: layer 5, written after `BoardCoordinates`, with two components in this order, `ChessMaterialComponent` and then a `UICanvasComponent` (`space="SceneView" useSafeArea="false"`; not modal, no `blocksInput`). **Elements:** two groups mirrored about the middle of the board, `MaterialTopList` with the balance label `MaterialTopBalance` and `MaterialBottomList` with `MaterialBottomBalance`; a list is five rows `Material<Top|Bottom><Queen|Rook|Bishop|Knight|Pawn>Row`, each an icon (`UIImageComponent`, sprite set in code) and a count label `{materialTopQueen}` etc. Every name starts with `Material` (elements are found by name in every canvas of the scene). **Words:** `OnDraw()` takes `bottom = BottomColor(...)` (live) and `SummarizeMaterial(model.GetPosition())` and, only when the model, its version or `bottom` changed, writes the twelve `UIVars` `material<Top|Bottom><Type>` ("x3", "" for none) and `material<Top|Bottom>Balance` (`BalanceText`: "+N" on the side that is ahead, otherwise ""); with no game all are empty. **Decisions:** (1) a row per piece type with a count, not an icon per piece (fixed rows can be measured). (2) The points are the conventional 1 / 3 / 3 / 5 / 9 of `Game/CapturedMaterial.h`, the balance is the difference on the board (a promotion counts) and the captured lists are position-only. (3) **Orientation:** the lower group is what the color at the bottom has taken, so the person's captures are always in the lower half and the groups swap when the board turns; a group shows what its color took, so its icons are the OTHER color's. (4) **"Too thin" is decided in view units:** `MaterialListFits` says whether the box of the lists lies inside the canvas's resolved `safeBounds`, asked on every draw (the safe area changes with the window, not with the model); a row shows only when its count is above 0 and the box fits. **Before the canvas has been laid out once the rows stay hidden**, so nothing flashes for a frame. In the narrowest window (480 px wide with side insets) the lists are hidden and the balance stays (the owner's decision). Held by `ChessUiTest` and `MaterialLayoutTest`. **Known limits:** (a) AutoFit rounds the smallest size up to a whole pixel, so in the narrowest window the balance can go down to 7 px (8 px measured for "+39" and "+99"); (b) the balance boxes end at x 100 and the rank labels start at x 102, so a "+39" beside a rank "8" may read as one number; (c) with a top notch the HUD moves down, and in 32 of the 46 notch cases of the size matrix (landscape and square) `Turn` is drawn over the first rows of the upper group **and over its balance `MaterialTopBalance`** (layer 10 over 5); the rule of the lists does not look at the HUD and the test asserts the overlap; the fix is the owner's call (moving the group breaks the mirror).
- **The HUD** is a `SceneView` canvas on the `Hud` node (layer 10, over the end-of-game dim), in the two margins of the view, every control 56 units high and anchored to a corner. Top row: `Turn` (label `{turn}`), `Menu` (button, the fixed text "Menu", action `ChessOpenOpponent`) and `Reset` (`ChessReset`). Bottom row: `Status` (label `{status}`, bottom right). `Menu` is **always shown** and is a fixed word, not a state: the owner did not want a button named after a state, so the choice is shown only in the panel it opens, and no element of the `Hud` is called `Opponent`. **Why one row per margin:** a 56-unit button is a 32 px touch target at the smallest fit scale (0.57); a second row needs a taller view and a smaller board. **The width budget** is the narrowest case of the size matrix (480 px with side insets, safe area about 689 units): the widest `Turn` sentence ("Computer is thinking") and the widest `Status` ("Engine not supported here") fit their boxes with little to spare, so a longer sentence needs a reword or a wider box; sentences say what went wrong and never what to do next (README, "Playing against the computer", does). **Thinking and errors:** "Computer is thinking" is the `Turn` label; the engine's error is the `Status` label (and the error line of the panel) and wins over "Check" while it lasts, `Turn` still says whose move it is. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` runs the HUD through the size matrix with every word the game can show, taken from the tables through `tests/HudWords.h` (never a copy; the font is proportional, so each word gets a round). `TextDoesNotFit` does not see an ellipsis, so the test asks `CheckContent().fits` of every control itself (8 units of air between a button's text and its fill) and demands 8 units between controls. `ChessHudWebTest` holds the same elements in HTML + CSS to the same rects.
- **The opponent panel** is the node `OpponentPanel` (layer 20, over the HUD and the result bar): a `SceneView` canvas started `visible="false"` and `modal="true"`, written after the engine's template for a choice panel (`engine/src/Modules/BECore/UI/CLAUDE.md`, "Choice panels"). A full-screen `Dim` (`blocksInput`) and a `Plate` (`anchor="Center" width="94%" maxWidth="640"`) with the `Title`, four rows, the `OpponentError` label (`{opponentError}`) and `Ok`. A row is a caption and a `UISegmented` group of 56-unit button segments (a segment needs an explicit `height`, a flex one gets height 0). The groups, with the ids of the control API as the `value` of their segments: `ModeGroup` (`two`, `computer`; `opponentMode`, `ChessSetMode`), `ColorGroup` (`white`, `black`; `opponentColor`, `ChessSetColor`), `EngineGroup` (`stockfish`, `own`; `opponentEngine`, `ChessSetEngine`), `LevelGroup` (`easy` .. `max`; `opponentLevel`, `ChessSetLevel`). Without Stockfish the segment `EngineStockfish` is hidden, so `EngineOwn` fills the group. `UISegmented` marks the segment whose `value` equals the bound variable as `selected` on every draw, and a press only emits its action: the group owns no choice, the board answers the action and publishes the variable, so the panel always shows what the game really has. **The color is the PERSON's color**; the control API's `opponent.plays` is the PROGRAM's, the opposite one, and the panel never shows `plays`. **A pick applies at once and the panel stays open**; only OK (`ChessCloseOpponent`) closes it (no Escape, no click outside). While it is shown the dim, the plate and the segments take every press, so the board and Reset get none. **No group is greyed:** a disabled group ate the click, which the owner read as a dead button; a pick made while two people play is remembered, shown as selected, and applies when Computer is picked. **A pick that fails is refused:** no silent fallback, and the pick does not switch the program off: "Computer" with no Stockfish found changes nothing (`opponentMode` stays `two`); a Stockfish pick while Own engine plays keeps Computer, Own engine, the level and the running search. The controller keeps the error, and "Engine not found" stands in the `OpponentError` line and in `Status` until a move of someone else, a reset, a `set_state` or the next pick; the way on is `Own engine`. **The names** are `ModeGroup` and so on, not `Mode`, `Engine`, `Level`, because elements are found by name in every canvas of the scene; a test checks that no name is in two canvases. The layout is held by `ChessUiTest` (the checks of the template on the shipped scene) and `ChessHudWebTest` (an HTML + CSS twin).
- **The promotion picker.** A click on a promotion target does not move the pawn and never plays the queen on its own: `ChessGameModel::OnSquareClicked` leaves the promotion **pending in the model** (`HasPendingPromotion()`, `GetPromotionTarget()`), and the person picks the piece in the node `PromotionPanel`. The pieces, ids and words are one table, `Game/PromotionChoices.*`. The panel is a modal `SceneView` canvas on layer 20, started `visible="false"`, written after the opponent panel: `PromotionDim` (`blocksInput`), `PromotionPlate` with `PromotionTitle`, four 56-unit buttons `PromoteQueen` / `PromoteRook` / `PromoteBishop` / `PromoteKnight` (`action="ChessPromote"`, the `value` is the id) and `PromotionCancel` (`ChessCancelPromotion`); names start with `Promotion` or `Promote`. **Shown by the model:** `ChessBoardComponent` shows the canvas while `HasPendingPromotion()` and hides it otherwise, after a click and on every `SceneUpdateEvent` (only when that differs from what it set last), so a Reset, a `set_state` or a move of the program or of the control API closes a stale panel; `ClearSelection()` drops the pending choice and so does any click on a square. `ChessPromote` makes the board play `ChoosePromotion(id)`; Cancel keeps the pawn selected, so the same target asks again. The control API, UCI and the program name the piece themselves (`e7e8q`) and never see the panel; the version does not move when a promotion becomes pending or is cancelled.
- **The drag.** A piece can be dragged to its target next to the two-click move; `ChessBoardComponent` does it; the model, the control API and the engine know nothing of it. **The press acts at once**, as before (`OnMouseDown` ends in `OnSquareClicked`; modal panels take the press, so no drag is armed). **Arming:** if the press was the LEFT button, the game is not over, no promotion is pending and the model has a selection on exactly the pressed square, the board keeps a `Drag`; every press clears it first. Past `kDragThreshold` from the press the drag is `lifted` for good; the threshold decides only the picture, never the move. **Release (left button):** over a legal target of the selected piece (`GetLegalMoves()`, mapped like the press, so the turned board works) the board calls `OnSquareClicked(target)`, the path of a second click (`TryMove`, or a pending promotion); anywhere else **nothing is called on the model**, so the piece goes back, stays selected and the move can be finished by a click, and a release on another own piece does not select it (the owner's decision). The drag is cleared in every case and on a motion without the button; `IsDragValid` (same model and version, not over, selection still on the square, no promotion, not the program's turn) drops it silently when a reset, a `set_state` or a move of someone else comes during it. **The picture:** `GetLiftedPiece()` returns `std::optional<LiftedPiece>` (`from`, the top-left in scene units: the pointer minus half a square), engaged only while the drag is armed, valid and lifted. `ChessPieceComponent` puts the piece there and moves its node to `kDragLayer` (6): over the pieces, the result bar and the material chips, under the HUD and the panels; the saved layer is given back in the first update that does not lift it. **A played drop does not slide**; the program's reply does. Held by `ChessUiTest`.
- **The words of the HUD** are `UIVars` that `ChessBoardComponent` publishes in `OnDraw()`, again whenever the model's version, the controller's `GetRevision()`, its `IsThinking()` or the person's color changed (the color changes while two people play with no other change). `turn` (`StatusText::Turn(model, thinking)`), `status` (the opponent's error sentence while one stands, `OpponentText::Error`, else the status of the game; the error wins over "Check" because the game is stalled by it; any act clears it) and `opponentError` (the same sentence while an error stands, else empty). The ids the panel groups are bound to, those of the control API: `opponentMode` (`two` / `computer`), `opponentColor` (**the person's color**), `opponentEngine`, `opponentLevel` (the picked ones, also while nobody plays). The words of the segments are static text of the scene, held to the tables by a test. `OnAction` answers `ChessReset`, `ChessOpenOpponent` / `ChessCloseOpponent` (show and hide the canvas `OpponentPanel`; a scene without it has nothing to show), `ChessSetMode` / `ChessSetColor` / `ChessSetEngine` / `ChessSetLevel` (pick through `ChessGame::GetActiveChoice()` with the event's `value`; an id in no table changes nothing and logs one warning; a refused pick is shown through `GetError()` and the variables), `ChessPromote` and `ChessCancelPromotion`.
- **Draw order.** `Board` has `layer="-1"`, the pieces stay on layer 0 and `Result` has `layer="1"`: squares and highlights first, sprites over them, the end-of-game dim over the board. `BoardCoordinates` is on layer -1 too and written right after `Board`: the same layer is drawn in scene order, so its glyphs come after the squares and before the pieces. `Material` is on layer 5, the HUD on 10, the panels on 20. The order comes from the layer, not from where the node sits in `SceneConfig.xml` (`engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`, "Draw order"); a test reads the recorded draw calls of one frame.
