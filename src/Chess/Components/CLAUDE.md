# Chess scene and UI (src/Chess/Components, config/SceneConfig.xml)

Part of the Chess documentation, split out of the root `CLAUDE.md` (which holds the overview, the control API, the rules and the map of these files) so that a session loads it only when it works here. A section named in quotes may be in a sibling file: the scene and the UI in `src/Chess/Components/CLAUDE.md`, the opponent in `src/Chess/Engine/CLAUDE.md`, the rules in `src/Chess/Game/CLAUDE.md`, the tests in `tests/CLAUDE.md`. **Read this before changing `config/SceneConfig.xml`**: the HUD, the panels, the coordinates and the captured material are described here.

## Files

```
└── Components/               scene glue (IComponent)
    ├── ChessBoardComponent   draws the board, the check, the hint and the highlights (from White's side, or turned by 180 degrees while the program plays White: `GetBottomColor()`, `SquareToPixel` and `OnMouseDown` go through `Game/BoardOrientation.h`), publishes the 16 variables of the edge labels (`coordFile0..7`, `coordRank0..7`, from the orientation it draws), turns mouse clicks, and drags (see "The drag"), into OnSquareClicked (and restarts after the game ends; ignores a click while a program plays the side to move: either seat, so a watch of two programs takes no click on either turn, keeps White at the bottom and leaves the captured-material groups as they are; the Reset button, a click after the end and Two players in the panel work as they do now), runs the opponent's Update() and the analyst's Update() (`ChessGame::GetAnalyst()`, the evaluation and the hint) on every SceneUpdateEvent and logs the result of a game once, publishes `turn`, `status`, `opponentMode`, `opponentColor`, `opponentEngine`, `opponentLevel` and `opponentError` to UIVars when the model, the opponent's revision, its search or the person's color changed, answers the actions ChessReset, ChessOpenOpponent / ChessCloseOpponent (show and hide the canvas of the node `OpponentPanel`) and ChessSetMode / ChessSetColor / ChessSetEngine / ChessSetLevel (the `value` of the event is an id, picked through `OpponentChoice`; an id in no table is ignored with one warning), ChessPromote (the `value` is the id of a piece of `PromotionChoices`; `ChoosePromotion` plays it, an id in no table is ignored with one warning) and ChessCancelPromotion (`CancelPromotion`); shows the canvas of the node `PromotionPanel` while the model has a pending promotion and hides it otherwise, after a click on the board and on every SceneUpdateEvent (so a reset, a `set_state` or a move of someone else closes it), only when that differs from what it set last
    ├── ChessMaterialComponent  on the node `Material` (layer 5), before its canvas: keeps no game state; in `OnDraw()` it publishes the twelve `material...` variables and gives the ten icons their sprites for the captured material of the model (`Game/CapturedMaterial.h`) and the orientation of the board, and shows or hides the ten rows (see "The captured material")
    ├── ChessPieceComponent   logical handle of one piece; drives its TransformComponent from the model (a played move is slid, see "How it works") and switches its sprite after a promotion
    └── ChessResultComponent  on its own node (layer 1, over the pieces): dims the board and draws the result bar once the game is over
```

## How it works

- A piece node in `ChessScene` is a `ChessPieceComponent` + `SpriteRendererComponent` (artwork from `TextureLibrary`, `config/TextureLibrary.xml`) + `TransformComponent`. `OnAttached` registers the piece with the model at its starting square (a piece that registers late, in the first frame, after a client has already moved, is dealt to the position as it stands: `RegisterPiece` re-deals when the game has left the opening); `Reset()` sends every handle back. The model moves a handle with its piece through captures, castling (the rook too), en passant (the passed pawn's handle is the one removed) and promotion (the handle reports the new type and the component switches the sprite to `<Color><Type>`, e.g. `WhiteQueen`, when the slide of the move is over).
- **A played move slides** (`ChessPieceComponent`, the animation is the component's only: the model, the control API and the version know nothing of it). Each update the component compares the model with what it saw last (its square, `GetMoves().size()`, `GetVersion()` and `GetStartFen()`); it is a **move** only when the count of moves and the version are each exactly one more and the start position is the same, the frame has time in it (`SceneUpdateEvent::deltaTime > 0`), `_slideDuration > 0` and the piece was seen before. Then a `ScopedTween` (`CoreManager::GetTweens()`, `QuadOut`) runs a **progress 0..1**, not a pixel, and the position is the `Lerp` of the pixels of the two squares, taken from the board's `SquareToPixel` on every update, so a board that turns or a changed geometry during a slide cannot leave a piece in a wrong place; the last value of the tween is exactly 1, so the piece ends on its cell. The tween first moves in the next frame (stage 2b), so the frame that sees the move still draws the old square. **Everything else is a snap**, as before: the first sighting (the pieces register in the first frames, also after moves played before them), `Reset()`, `SetPosition` / `set_state`, a reset and moves between two frames (the version tells it from one move), a count that falls, the model or the board gone; a snap also ends a slide under way. **A frame with no time snaps** (`deltaTime` 0: a bare `UpdateAll()`, a `screenshot` frame of the manual clock, `step` with `dt` 0), which is why `ChessUiTest` and the manual-clock pictures of the smoke need no waiting; a slide already running stays where it is in such a frame. **A captured piece** (and the pawn taken en passant) stays on its square for the same time and is then moved off-screen (`-10000`): the sprite has no alpha, so there is no fade. A castling rook slides with its king. A promotion changes the sprite when the slide ends. **The slide time is `_slideDuration`** (reflected, seconds, default 0.15, `0` = no slide, always on, no switch for the person): it is a field of each piece node, so the scene editor tunes one node and all 32 cannot be tuned at once; `SceneConfig.xml` does not set it (the default lives in `kDefaultSlideDuration`). A second move while a slide runs replaces it and the new one starts from the square the piece was last on (a jump of a few frames at most). **Known limits:** a takeback (`chess-move-list-takeback`) is not `+1` move, so it snaps; a piece passes under or over its neighbours in scene order (layer 0 draws in scene order and there is no layer between the pieces and the result dim), so a knight can pass under a later piece; clicks during a slide work on the model as before. Held by `ChessPieceAnimationTest` (a driven clock).
- **The node is the whole square, the sprite is fitted inside it.** `ChessPieceComponent` writes the node's position and `_width = _height = squareSize` every update, and the sprite of each of the 32 piece nodes is `fit="Contain"` (`SpriteFit`, `engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`): the piece is drawn inside the square with the aspect of its atlas cell (116.67 x 190.5, tall), as high as the square and centered. The board fills the selection, legal-move and check highlights over the same full square, so they sit around the piece and `ChessBoardComponent` does not need to know the size of the piece. A new piece node needs the attribute (`Stretch`, the default, squashes the tall cell into the square; a test counts the 32).
- The piece atlas (`assets/chess_test.png`) must keep a real alpha channel: a sprite with an opaque background paints a box over its square and hides the square color and the highlights. A replacement atlas needs a transparent background. Its cells may have any aspect: the fit keeps whatever `srcRect` the library gives, so the cell size in `TextureLibrary.xml` and the art can change without touching the scene.
- Board geometry (`_originX`, `_originY`, `_squareSize`) is reflected so it can be tuned in the scene editor. It is in scene units, not window pixels: `ChessScene` declares `viewWidth="840" viewHeight="800"` (the board 640x640 at (100, 80): 100 units on the sides and 80 above and below, where the HUD sits), so the engine scales the whole scene to the window and centers it. Change the view size together with the board geometry, and map mouse positions with `Scene::ScreenToSceneX/Y` (`OnMouseDown` does). **The board is drawn from Black's side exactly when the program plays White** (mode Computer and the person's color Black; `BottomColor(OpponentController::GetPlays())`): the 8x8 grid is turned by 180 degrees (a1 at the top right, h8 at the bottom left, the files run h..a from the left), the pieces stay upright and the person's pieces are at the bottom. Two people keep White at the bottom, and so does the program as Black; a color picked while two people play turns nothing until Computer is picked, and a refused pick changes nothing. `ChessBoardComponent::GetBottomColor()` reads the opponent **live on every use** (no cached flag, so it follows the panel, `set_state` of the control API and Reset, which keeps the opponent), `SquareToPixel` (so the pieces, the squares and the highlights) maps a square to its cell with `CellOf`, and `OnMouseDown` maps the clicked cell back with `SquareAt`. The dim and the result bar are symmetric about the board and the HUD and the panels are fixed on the screen, so they do not change. Only the geometry turns: the model, the control API (`get_state.board` stays rank 8 first), UCI and FEN do not.
- **The coordinates** are the file letters and rank numbers drawn on the board, shown by default. The renderer has no text primitive, so they are the 16 `UILabel`s of a `SceneView` canvas on the node `BoardCoordinates` (`layer="-1"`, after `Board`; `useSafeArea="false"`, because the board is at fixed scene units and an inset must not move a label; not interactive and no `blocksInput`, so the board still gets every click, also on a corner cell). **Where:** `RankLabel0..7` (row from the top) in the left column of cells, top-left in the cell; `FileLabel0..7` (column from the left) in the bottom row, bottom-right in the cell, the way lichess does. A box is 13 x 26 units in the 13-unit corner of the 80-unit cell (x 2..15 for a rank, 65..78 for a file) at font 18 (about 10 px at the narrowest fit scale, 0.57; the line of an 18-unit font is about 24 units high, so a box of 22 cut the glyph and the matrix said so); a `Contain`-fitted piece covers about 15.5..64.5 of its cell (the aspect of the atlas cell, 116.67 x 190.5), so a label never reaches a piece. The highlights lie under the labels (same layer, drawn earlier). **The rects are fixed on the screen and the text turns:** each label shows `{coordRank<row>}` / `{coordFile<column>}`, `UIVars` that `ChessBoardComponent::OnDraw()` publishes from `Game/BoardCoordinates.h` (`FileLabel(column, bottom)`, `RankLabel(row, bottom)` for the `bottom` color it draws with), before the model check (they show also when no game is active) and only when that color changed since the last publication (`_publishedBottom`, a `std::optional<PieceColor>`, so a label never re-lays out for nothing; the interned names are built once). With the program as White the bottom reads h..a and the left 1..8 from the top, with two people or the program as Black a..h and 8..1; the fixed boxes never move. **The contrast is static:** the turn by 180 degrees keeps the parity of the grid (a1 is dark in both orientations), so the XML gives the labels the dark brown of the dark squares (`181,136,99`) on a light cell and the cream (`240,217,181`) on a dark one (a cell is light when column + row is even; the rank labels are in column 0, the file labels in row 7), and `IsLightSquare` is the one function the board paints and the labels' test checks with. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` runs the canvas through the validator and `CheckContent().fits` on the size matrix and in the narrowest window for all 8 rounds of letters (every letter and digit at every label), checks every rect against its edge cell and the width a piece covers (and that width against a real piece draw), the colors against the squares a frame really fills, the draw order and that a hit test finds none of the 16, and builds broken copies from the text of the shipped XML (a font of 60, a box of 3 or 30 units, a label under a cell that is not at the edge, a rank number at the bottom of its cell, a file letter at the top of its cell, a wrong color, a missing label) that fail the checks. **Not done:** no switch to hide them (an owner's call: the margins of the HUD are full, so it would go into the panel of the Menu button), no web twin in `ChessHudWebTest` (the file holds the HUD and the opponent panel), and the look in a real window has been seen only in tests (size and weight of the glyphs at small windows, the two colors on the selection and legal-move highlights).
- **The hint on the board.** While the analyst holds a hint for the position on the board (`PositionAnalyst::GetHint(model)`: asked at this version, answered for it; the control API's `set_state {"analysis": {"hint": true}}` asks for one, the HUD button comes in a later slice of `chess-analysis-hints`) and the game is not over, `ChessBoardComponent::OnDraw()` fills the two squares of its move: `kHintFromSquare` (blue, alpha 110) on the square the piece leaves and `kHintToSquare` (the same blue, alpha 200) on the one it goes to, with the same `SquareToPixel(..., bottom, ...)` as every other highlight, so they follow the turned board. **Draw order inside the board:** squares, the check, the hint, then the selection and the legal moves, so a selection stays readable on top of it; the coordinates' glyphs (layer -1, after `Board`) and then the pieces (layer 0) are drawn over all of them. Nothing is played and the model, the pieces and `get_state.board` do not change. **When it goes:** the hint belongs to the model's version, so a move (by mouse, by the control API or by the program), a reset, a `set_state` or a `SetPosition` take it off at the next frame, and a finished game shows none; a click that only selects a piece is not a change of the position and keeps it. While the engine is still thinking, or has failed, nothing is drawn. With no game active the board draws only its squares. **The two colors are a first guess nobody has seen on screen** (contrast on light and dark squares, next to the selection green and the red of check): the owner may retune the constants. Held by `ChessUiTest` (the hint tests at its end) and, headless, by a screenshot comparison in `control_smoke.py`.
- **The captured material** is what each side has taken and who is ahead, shown in the **left** margin of the view (x 0 to 100) by the node `Material`: layer 5 (over the pieces and the result bar, under the HUD and the panels), written after `BoardCoordinates`, with two components in this order, `ChessMaterialComponent` and then a `UICanvasComponent` (`space="SceneView" useSafeArea="false"`, fixed scene units like the coordinates; not modal, no `blocksInput`, nothing interactive, so the board and the Reset button get every click and a hit test finds none of the elements). The component comes first because components of a node draw in order: it gives the variables their words and the rows their state before the canvas draws, in the same frame. **Elements:** two groups mirrored about the middle of the board. The upper group is the list `MaterialTopList` (x 8, y 84, 64 x 216, a vertical `UIStack`, spacing 4, rows stretched) and the balance label `MaterialTopBalance` (x 77, y 84, 23 x 28); the lower one `MaterialBottomList` and `MaterialBottomBalance` at y 500 (the lists end at 300 and 716). A list holds five rows in the order queen, rook, bishop, knight, pawn, `Material<Top|Bottom><Queen|Rook|Bishop|Knight|Pawn>Row`: a horizontal stack 40 high (padding 2, spacing 4, a cream chip fill `232,210,170`) of a `...Icon` (`UIImage`, 26 wide; the sprite name is set in code, the image keeps the aspect of its atlas cell) and a `...Count` (`UILabel`, `{materialTopQueen}` etc., font 20, centered, `Clip`, a dark brown `70,45,30`). The rows start `visible="false"`, and a hidden row takes no room in the stack, so the rows of the pieces that are gone pack at the top of their list. Every name starts with `Material` (elements are found by name in every canvas of the scene). **Words:** `ChessMaterialComponent::OnDraw()` reads `ChessGame::GetActiveModel()` and `GetActiveOpponent()`, takes `bottom = BottomColor(opponent->GetPlays())` (the board's own function, read live on every draw) and `SummarizeMaterial(model.GetPosition())`, and only when the model, its version or `bottom` changed since its last publication writes the twelve `UIVars` `material<Top|Bottom><Queen|Rook|Bishop|Knight|Pawn>` ("x3", or "" for none) and `material<Top|Bottom>Balance` (`BalanceText` of the lead of the color of that group: "+N" on the side that is ahead, "" on the other and at equality, "+99" at most); with no game active the summary is empty, so all twelve are empty and no row is shown. **Orientation (decision 4):** the lower group is what the color at the bottom of the board has taken, the upper group what the other color has taken, so the groups swap when the board turns (the program as White) and the person's captures are always in the lower half. A group shows what its color took, so the icon sprites are the OTHER color's, `WhitePawn` ... `BlackQueen` in the names of `TextureLibrary.xml`; they are set when the bottom color changes (and in the first publication). **Decisions of the entry, with their reasons:** (1) one row per piece type with a count ("x2"), not one icon per captured piece: a captured set is at most 15 pieces, fixed rows need 10 static names and the count is a plain `UIVars` word, so the layout is a small set of elements that can be measured; an icon per piece (the lichess look) needs 30 slots whose sprite is set in code and can replace the rows later without touching `Game/`. (2) The points are the conventional 1 / 3 / 3 / 5 / 9 of `Game/CapturedMaterial.h`, not the centipawns of `Engine/Evaluation.h`. (3) The balance is the difference of the points on the board, so a promotion counts (a pawn that became a queen is +8); the captured lists are position-only (the rule of `CapturedMaterial.h`: a piece beyond the starting set is a promoted pawn, it counts in the points and is not a missing pawn; a pawn that promoted into a piece that was missing shows as a pawn gone; a promoted piece that is taken later shows as a missing pawn). (4) The orientation above. (5) **"Too thin" is decided in view units from the resolved safe area of the canvas:** `Game/MaterialLayout.h` has the box of the two lists, (8, 84) to (72, 716), and `MaterialListFits` says whether it lies inside `safeBounds`; the component asks it on every draw (the safe area changes with the window, not with the model), with `canvas->GetLayout().GetResolved().safeBounds`, and shows a row only when its count is above 0 and the box fits, setting `UICanvasComponent::SetElementVisible` only for the rows whose state differs from the last one it set. **Before the canvas has been laid out once, the safe area is not known and the rows stay hidden**, so no row flashes for one frame (the first frame has the words, the next one the rows); a resize is seen on the draw after it, in both directions. In the narrowest window of the size matrix (480 px wide with side insets of 9 %, scale 0.571) the safe area starts at x = 75.6, about 24 units of the margin are left, and the lists are hidden (the owner's decision of 2026-11-07: the balance stays); the balance boxes (x 77 to 100) are never hidden by this rule. **The balance in that window:** the box is 23 x 28 units, 13.1 x 16 px, and the label has `overflow="AutoFit"` with `minFontSize="12"`: measured while the tests were written, "+1" and "+9" are drawn at 11 px and "+39" and "+99" at 8 px (14 units, so the 12 units of `minFontSize` are not reached), the latter 12.8 of the 13.1 px wide; the tests demand that the drawn text is neither cut nor under the smallest whole size (7 px: AutoFit rounds `minFontSize * scale` up) and that `CheckContent().fits` is true for every sign from "+1" to "+99" in both boxes. It fits with little to spare and 8 px is small; nobody has seen it in a window. **The right margin is left free** for the move list and takeback of the entry `chess-move-list-takeback` (the owner wants the list on one side and the material on the other). **The look is a first guess:** the chip is a cream a little darker than the light squares (so that a test can tell a chip from a square in the recorded draw calls), the text color is none of the two colors of the board's labels (so the coordinate test does not take the counts for its glyphs), and the sizes of the font and the pictures at this size have been seen only in tests. **Held by tests** (`ChessUiTest`, `MaterialLayoutTest`; the rows of `ChessUiTest` in the tests table): the node and the order of its components, the elements, their names and their order, the rect of each list and balance, the words and the rows after a capture by clicks, a turned board, a color picked in the panel, a reset, a `set_state`, a promotion, a move of the program and no active game, nothing taking a click, the draw order of a frame, the narrowest window and a resize in both directions, the whole canvas on the size matrix with every count word and every sign, the shown rects against the margin, the HUD and the coordinate labels, the aspect of an icon, and broken copies that fail the checks. The control smoke reads `get_state.material` and, headless, the margin of a screenshot. **How the layout is held:** the validator and `MaterialProblems` on the 138 cases of the matrix for 99 rounds of words with the rows shown by hand (counts "x1" to "x8": a row shows at most the eight pawns, and "x10" does not fit the 30-unit count box at font 20; signs "+1" to "+99"), the narrowest window alone, and broken copies of the shipped XML. The stop rule of the plan (if a sign cannot fit the balance box at the smallest size, report instead of widening the box over the board or dropping the balance) was not triggered: every sign fits. **Known limits:** (a) AutoFit rounds the smallest size up to a whole pixel, so the balance can go down to 7 px (`ceil(12 * 0.571)`), one below the 8 px measured for "+39" and "+99"; (b) a window with a top notch pushes the HUD down, and in 32 of the 46 notch cases of the matrix (the landscape and square ones) the `Turn` label (x 12 to 332, y 68 to 124, in a square window y 50.8 to 106.8) is drawn over the first rows of the upper group **and over its balance `MaterialTopBalance`** (x 77 to 100, y 84 to 112; layer 10 over layer 5), so the "+N" that the owner's decision keeps is read under "White to move" there; the same kind of overlap the HUD has with the board in those windows ("The HUD" above), and the rule of the lists does not look at the HUD (the test asserts the overlap of the balance and the first row in each of the 32 cases, which is the limit written down, not a requirement); (c) the balance boxes end at x 100 and the rank labels of the board start at x 102, so a "+39" beside a rank "8" may read as one number (2 units apart; the owner's call on screen). **Not done:** the notch overlap of limit (b) is not fixed (the owner's call: moving the upper group below y 132, the worst bottom edge of `Turn` plus 8 units, breaks the mirror about the middle of the board, so the lower group would move to about y 452 to 668, or the upper group is hidden in the notch windows); no switch to hide the material (it would go into the panel of the Menu button), no web twin in `ChessHudWebTest` (that file holds the HUD and the opponent panel), no icon per piece (rows with a count), the look in a real window has been seen only in tests, the lists are position-only (a promoted piece that is taken shows as a missing pawn; "+99" is the most the balance says), and the right margin is reserved for `chess-move-list-takeback`.
- **The HUD** is a `SceneView` canvas on the `Hud` node (layer 10, over the end-of-game dim), in the two margins of the view, 12 units from the edge of the safe area, every control 56 units high and anchored to a corner. Top row: `Turn` (label `{turn}`, left, 320 wide, font 28), `Menu` (button, the fixed text "Menu", action `ChessOpenOpponent`, `anchor="TopRight" x="-134"`, 110 wide, font 24, blue) and `Reset` (button, `ChessReset`, 110 wide, font 26, green). Bottom row: `Status` (label `{status}`, bottom right, `48%` wide, right aligned, font 28) alone; the bottom left is empty. `Menu` is **always shown** and is a fixed word, not a state; one press opens the opponent panel (next bullet), which shows what is picked now. **The choice is not shown on the HUD outside the panel** (an owner-question default of 2026-11-06: the panel shows it; moving it to `Turn` or `Status`, or a wider `Menu`, is a later call): the owner did not want a button named after a state ("Two players"), so the button says what it does. The element was `Opponent` and carried `{side}` ("Two players" / "You: White" / "You: Black", 190 wide); the variable `side` and `OpponentText::SideLabel` are gone, and no element of the `Hud` canvas is called `Opponent`. The HUD scales and moves with the board. That it stays outside the board is checked in five window shapes without a safe-area inset (`TheHudStaysOutsideTheBoardWithoutInsets`); with a notch or a home-bar inset in a landscape or square window a row can reach onto the edge of the board (the old HUD did too). **Why one row per margin:** the margin is 80 units, a 56-unit button is a 32 px touch target at the smallest fit scale of the size matrix (0.57, because the view is 840 units wide); a second row would need a taller view and a smaller board. **The width budget** is set by the narrowest case of the matrix, a 480 px wide window with side insets (9 % each side): scale 0.571, a safe area of about 689 units. Top row: `Turn` ends at 332, `Menu` spans about 445 to 555 and `Reset` 567 to 677 (gaps of 113 and 12; the gap to `Reset` is the one the 190-unit button had); "Computer is thinking" needs 249 of the 320, and `ChessUiTest` lays "Menu" into the 110 units of the button with 8 units of air (it needs far less than the old three words). Bottom row: `Status` is about 330.7 wide from about 346; the widest sentence is "Engine not supported here" at 318.9 of 330.6 ("Draw: insufficient material" is close behind; the old "Engine made an illegal move" needed 347 and was reworded to "Illegal engine move"). Those numbers were measured when the bottom row also held two buttons: the geometry and the words of `Turn`, `Reset` and `Status` have not changed since, and `Menu` keeps the right edge of the old `Level` button (narrower, 110 wide against 190); `Status` keeps its `48%` although the bottom row is free now, and nobody has re-measured. A wider window only adds room (the anchors are corners and `Status` is a percent), so the narrowest case decides. **Thinking and errors:** "Computer is thinking" is the `Turn` label and "Black to move" comes back when the answer is played; the engine's error is the `Status` label (and the error line of the panel) and wins over "Check" while it lasts. While an error stands `Turn` still says whose move it is ("Black to move") and only `Status` explains; the sentence says what went wrong and never what to do next (the README section "Playing against the computer" does: Reset, "Two players" in the panel, the path). The window never freezes without a word: a search says so, a failure says so, and Reset, a pick in the panel or a move of someone else lets the person out. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` runs the HUD through the size matrix with every word the game can show, and the words come from the tables through `tests/HudWords.h` (`StatusText` over a scratch game, `OpponentText`, every `EngineErrorKind`), never a copy: the font is proportional, so the longest word by characters is not always the widest and each word gets a round. The validator's `TextDoesNotFit` does not see a label that cuts its text with an ellipsis (a label with `Ellipsis` "can shrink"), so the test asks `CheckContent().fits` of every shown control itself (a button must also keep 8 units of air between its text and the edge of its fill) and demands 8 units between any two controls. `ChessHudWebTest` holds the same four elements (`#Menu` among them) written in HTML + CSS to the same rects.
- **The opponent panel** is the node `OpponentPanel` (layer 20, over the HUD and the result bar): a `SceneView` canvas started `visible="false"` and `modal="true"`, written after the engine's template for a choice panel (`engine/tests/BECore/UISelectorScreenTest.cpp`, `kSelectorXml`; `engine/src/Modules/BECore/UI/CLAUDE.md`, "Choice panels"). A full-screen `Dim` (`blocksInput`) and a `Plate` (`anchor="Center" width="94%" maxWidth="640"`, 460 units high: 16 + 44 title + 4 x 56 rows + 32 error line + 56 OK + 6 x 12 spacing + 16; the view is a fixed 840 x 800, so there is no portrait variant) with the `Title`, four rows, the `OpponentError` label (height 32, font 22, red text `{opponentError}`, centered) and `Ok`. A row is a caption (150 wide) beside a `UISegmented` group of 56-unit `UIButton` segments (a segment needs an explicit `height`: a flex segment without one gets a rect of height 0). The groups, with the ids of the control API as the `value` of their segments: `ModeGroup` ("Opponent": `Two players` = `two`, `Computer` = `computer`; bind `opponentMode`, action `ChessSetMode`), `ColorGroup` ("Play as": `White` = `white`, `Black` = `black`; `opponentColor`, `ChessSetColor`), `EngineGroup` ("Engine": `Stockfish` = `stockfish`, `Own engine` = `own`; `opponentEngine`, `ChessSetEngine`) and `LevelGroup` ("Level": `Easy`, `Medium`, `Hard`, `Max` = `easy` .. `max`; `opponentLevel`, `ChessSetLevel`). The engine's `UISegmented` marks, on every draw, the segment whose `value` equals the bound variable as `selected`, and a press on a segment only emits its action with its `value`: the group owns no choice, the board answers the action and publishes the variable, so the panel always shows what the game really has. The shipped XML sets no `selectedColor`: the look of the selected segment is the engine's derived default, a first guess nobody has seen in a window yet. **The color is the PERSON's color**; the control API's `opponent.plays` is the PROGRAM's, the opposite one (the program plays Black = the person has White), and the panel never shows `plays`. **A pick applies at once and the panel stays open** (each press is one pick on the `OpponentController`: a new engine, the running search dropped, by the controller's own rules); only OK (`ChessCloseOpponent`) closes it. The engine's `UISegmented::disabled` stays a feature of the widget, this game does not use it. **A pick that fails is refused:** an engine that cannot be had cannot be saved (no fallback, nothing switches the engine silently, and the pick does not switch the program off either): "Computer" with no Stockfish found changes nothing, so `opponentMode` stays `two` (the screen shows the truth); a Stockfish pick while Own engine plays as Computer keeps Computer, Own engine, the level and the running search (an owner's decision of 2026-11-05: choosing Stockfish used to switch the mode to Two players). - **The promotion picker.** A click on a promotion target does not move the pawn and never plays the queen on its own: `ChessGameModel::OnSquareClicked` leaves the promotion **pending in the model** (state of the game, not of a component; `HasPendingPromotion()`, `GetPromotionTarget()`), and the person picks the piece in the node `PromotionPanel`. The pieces, their ids and their words are one table, `Game/PromotionChoices.*` (`queen`, `rook`, `bishop`, `knight`; "Queen", "Rook", "Bishop", "Knight"; queen first), which the scene is held to. - **The drag.** A piece can be dragged to its target next to the two-click move; `ChessBoardComponent` does the logic (it subscribes to `SDLEvents::MouseButtonUpEvent` and `MouseMotionEvent` too; nothing of the model, the control API or the engine knows of it). **The press acts at once**, exactly as before (`OnMouseDown` ends in `OnSquareClicked`: the clicks, the guard of the program's turn, the click after the end that starts a new game, and the modal panels, whose canvases take the press so that no drag is armed). **Arming:** after that, if the press was the LEFT button, the game is not over, no promotion is pending and the model has a selection on exactly the pressed square (the press selected or re-selected a piece of the side to move; a press that played a move leaves none), the board keeps a `Drag` (the square, the model, `GetVersion()` at the press, the press point and the pointer in scene units, `lifted` false). Every press clears the drag first. **Motion:** with no left button in `state` the drag is cleared (a release that a canvas took, over a HUD button for example, never reaches the board, and the next motion over the board cleans up; a motion over a button of the HUD is taken by its canvas and cleans up nothing, the next press does); otherwise the pointer is stored and past `kDragThreshold` (6 scene units) from the press the drag is `lifted` for good (a click with a shaky hand never lifts a piece). The threshold decides only the picture, never the move. **Release (left button), decided by the owner on 2026-11-07:** over a legal target of the selected piece (`GetLegalMoves()`, the square mapped by the same `SquareUnder` as the press, so the turned board works) the board calls `OnSquareClicked(target)`, the very path of a second click (`TryMove`, or a promotion left pending: the panel opens as after a click and the pawn stays on its square until a piece is picked); anywhere else (the start square, any other square, another own piece, off the board, the margins) **nothing is called on the model**, so the piece goes back and stays selected with its legal moves shown and the move can be finished by a click, and a release on another own piece does not select it. The drag is cleared in every case. **Valid** (`IsDragValid`): the same model, an unchanged version, not over, the selection still on the dragged square, no promotion pending and the program not to move (`PlaysFor`); otherwise the drag is dropped silently (a reset, a `set_state`, a control-API move or the program's move during the drag). A stuck drag is impossible: a release, a motion without the button, the next press, a failed check and the destruction of the board clear it. **For the picture** `GetLiftedPiece()` returns `std::optional<LiftedPiece>` (`from`, `x`, `y` = the top-left of the piece in scene units, the pointer minus half a square), engaged only while the drag is armed, valid and lifted; the board keeps no node or sprite. **Not done:** touch, a drag with the right button, Escape to cancel, a ghost on the origin square, a highlight of the square under the pointer and a cursor change; the piece does not follow the pointer yet (slice 2 of `chess-drag-and-drop`). Held by `ChessUiTest` (the `Drag` cases).
