# Chess opponent (src/Chess/Engine)

Part of the Chess documentation, split out of the root `CLAUDE.md` (which holds the overview, the control API, the rules and the map of these files) so that a session loads it only when it works here. A section named in quotes may be in a sibling file: the scene and the UI in `src/Chess/Components/CLAUDE.md`, the opponent in `src/Chess/Engine/CLAUDE.md`, the rules in `src/Chess/Game/CLAUDE.md`, the tests in `tests/CLAUDE.md`.

│   ├── Search.*              `Search(position, SearchSettings, SearchControl)`: iterative deepening negamax with alpha-beta, quiescence (captures, promotions, check evasions) and a one-ply check extension; best move of the last finished iteration first, then MVV-LVA, promotions, the two killers of the ply, the other quiet moves by a history table, then generator order (a stable sort; `SearchSettings::quietOrdering`, default on, switched off is the order without killers and history, kept for the tests; killers and history are learned in one call on a beta cutoff of a quiet move and used in the full-width nodes, not at the root or in quiescence); a transposition table (`SearchSettings::tableEntries`, default `kDefaultTableEntries` = 2^18 entries = 4 MB, 0 = none; built by the call for `maxDepth` of 3 or more, so easy and medium allocate nothing, and freed with it; the full-width nodes probe it for a cutoff or a narrower window when its depth is at least the node's, and take its move as the first to try; the root and quiescence do not use it); `SearchSettings::excludedRootMoves` (default empty) leaves moves out of the root list only, never touching the table, killers or history, so an empty list is the old search bit for bit (the node counts pinned in `SearchTest` are the guard); the lines after the first of `OwnEngine::Analyse` use it; mate scores carry the distance (the table keeps a mate as "m plies from this node", `ScoreToTable` / `ScoreFromTable`, so an entry read at another ply is right); draws in the tree (stalemate, insufficient material, 50 moves, a repeated position with the game's history); **the table and the draws:** a node whose subtree returned a repetition or 50-move draw stores nothing, a table result is taken (and stored) only where `HalfmoveClock() + depth + 32 < 100` so no 50-move draw can fall inside, and no state crosses calls; the graph history interaction (a result stored where a descendant could not repeat, read where it could) is not cured, the tests are a guard; stops on a `CancellationToken`, a deadline or a node limit, polled every `kSearchPollInterval` nodes, and hands the position back equal. **Deterministic for a fixed depth** (a table owned by one call, no randomness, no clock): the same position and depth give the same move, score and node count on every platform; a search cut by a deadline or a token is not deterministic.
│   ├── TranspositionTable.*  `TranspositionTable(entries)`: the fixed-size table of search results over the Zobrist key, pure; `Search` builds one per call (see its line). Entries rounded up to a power of two (at least 1), allocated once, never grown; a slot is the key masked by the size; `TableEntry` is 16 bytes (key, `Move` as an ordering hint, `int16_t` score, `int8_t` depth, `TableBound` `Exact` / `Lower` / `Upper`; an empty slot has depth -1). `Probe(key)` is null on a slot of another key. `Store(key, move, score, depth, bound)` is depth-preferred: another key is evicted only by an equal or deeper result, the same key is always replaced and keeps its old move when the new one is null. `ScoreToTable(score, ply)` / `ScoreFromTable(score, ply)` keep a mate score as "mates in m plies from this node" so an entry read at another ply gives the right distance from the root. Not thread safe: one table belongs to one search
│   ├── OwnEngine.*           `OwnEngine`: `IChessEngine` over `Search`, in the process of the game and on every target, with the contract of `UciEngine` minus the process (`BadRequest`, `NoMove`, `Cancelled`, a deadline is not an error; the move is checked against the generator before it leaves). `SearchLimits::depth` is the last iteration (deterministic), `moveTimeMs` a deadline (not deterministic), both: the earlier. No state between calls, so threads may share one object. **`Analyse(request, token)`** answers `min(request.lines, legal moves)` lines. Line 1: the best move of the last iteration that finished with its score from the side to move (`ScoreFromSearch`) and that depth, the search of a request for one line (at a fixed depth: same move, score, depth and node count). Line k = 2..N: a fresh `Search` at the same depth with the moves of lines 1..k-1 excluded at the root (`SearchSettings::excludedRootMoves`), with its own table, killers and history, so line 1 and the determinism for a depth are untouched; the scores of the lines are not forced to fall. A move time is shared out: line k of N must end by `start + moveTimeMs * k / N` (a first guess; the levels of the own engine use a depth); a token cancelled after any of the searches is `Cancelled` and the whole answer is dropped. `FindMove` and `Analyse` share `Prepare` (validate, `NoMove`, early `Cancelled`) and `SearchOnce` (the search, the retry, the legality check), so they give the same move for the same request and the same errors, deterministic for a depth, a deadline is not an error. **Picked by the person** in the opponent panel, the Engine row (or `opponent.engine` of the control API); the default engine is still Stockfish and nothing falls back to it: see "The opponent in the game"
│   ├── OpponentChoice.*      OpponentChoice: the choice as the opponent panel makes it, over an `OpponentController&` (no scene): `OpponentMode` (`Two`, `Computer`; ids "two", "computer"), the color of the PERSON (ids "white", "black"; the control API's `plays` is the PROGRAM's color, the opposite one), the engine and the level by their ids of the control API; SetMode / SetColor / SetEngine / SetLevel(id) -> `std::expected<void, ChoiceError>` (`BadId`: the id is in no table, nothing changed and no engine asked; `Engine`: the controller refused the pick, its `EngineError` inside), Mode / Color / ModeId / ColorId / EngineId / LevelId, Reset (White); `PieceColorId` and `ParseColor` are shared with `ChessGameControl`; see "The choice of the person"

## How it works

- **The opponent** (`Engine/`). `IChessEngine::FindMove` is synchronous and blocking: it returns when the engine has answered, was cancelled (`CancellationToken`) or failed, so a caller that must not wait runs it on a worker. It takes a `SearchRequest` (the start FEN of the game and the moves played, plus the limits), not a `Position`: UCI engines see repetitions only through `position fen <startFen> moves ...`, which a bare `Position` cannot give (`SearchRequest::FromPosition` drops the history, `FromModel` keeps it). Errors are values, `std::expected<Move, EngineError>`; a move that comes back is legal in the position the request leads to. `UciProtocol` writes the same `position` command as `position_command` of `tools/stockfish_bridge.py`. `UciProcess` is the child process under it (POSIX; Windows answers `Unsupported`): the child gets pipes for stdin and stdout, `/dev/null` for stderr and no other descriptor (the control socket of the game stays out of it; this holds on macOS and with glibc 2.34 or later, on older glibc and musl a descriptor of the game without close-on-exec is inherited, and Linux is not built or tested here), and a write to a dead child is `ProcessDied`, not `SIGPIPE` (on macOS the pipe carries `F_SETNOSIGPIPE`; elsewhere `Start()` sets `SIGPIPE` to ignored for the whole process, which the header says).
- **The UCI engine** (`UciEngine`) is `IChessEngine` over `UciProcess`, the port of `UciEngine` of the bridge. **The path comes from the caller** (`UciEngineSettings::launch`); `FindStockfish(path)` only looks for one, like `find_stockfish` of the bridge: the explicit path (it must be a file; when it is not there is no fallback), then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`. Nothing here knows where Stockfish is installed and nothing of it is shipped. Stockfish is GPL-3.0: it is neither linked nor copied, none of its code or files enter this repository or `engine/`, it is a separate program the user installs and the game starts through a path. The constructor starts nothing; the process starts on the first `FindMove` or `Analyse` and again on the first one after an error closed it. One search at a time (a mutex); the waits go in slices of 20 ms, so a cancelled `CancellationToken` is seen soon. Failures are `EngineError` kinds: `BadRequest` (limits or request wrong; no process is started), `NoMove` (the game is over, which the engine is not asked, or it answered `bestmove (none)` / `0000`), `IllegalMove` (a move the position does not allow, its text in `detail`), `LaunchFailed`, `Timeout` (no `uciok` / `readyok` within `startupTimeoutMs`, no `bestmove` within `movetime + answerSlackMs`, or `depthOnlyTimeoutMs` for a depth without time), `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError`, `Cancelled` (`stop` is sent and the answer to it is read and dropped), `Unsupported` (Windows: `UciProcess` has no implementation there; it is also the default answer of an `IChessEngine` that does not analyse, which `UciEngine` does not give). **What closes the process** (`quit`, `quitGraceMs`, then `SIGTERM` and `SIGKILL`; always reaped): `Timeout`, `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError` (not `Analyse`'s missing score: the engine answered, so the process is kept), a cancelled search whose engine does not answer `stop` within `stopGraceMs`, a cancel during the setup (`uciok` / `readyok`, `ucinewgame` + `isready`: no search runs yet, so no `stop` is sent, the process is closed and `Cancelled` comes back once the process has gone: at once for an engine that obeys `quit`, after the `quitGraceMs` and the signals for one that does not), and the destructor (on the thread that destroys the engine, up to the `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s for the signals). `NoMove`, `IllegalMove`, the `ProtocolError` of an `Analyse` with no usable score and a cancelled search the engine did answer keep it. `ucinewgame` goes with the first search of a process, another start FEN, or moves that do not extend the previous request's. **`Analyse(request, token)`** is `FindMove` with a score: both run one private `RunSearch` (validation, `NoMove` before any process, the early `Cancelled`, `Begin`, the wait for `bestmove` with `StopSearch` / `CloseAndFail`, the move checked against the generator), so the two send the same `position` and `go`, have the same deadlines, errors, process rules and the same `ucinewgame` bookkeeping (a call of one then the other on one object is one game). While the wait for `bestmove` runs, every skipped `info` line goes through `UciProtocol::ParseInfo`; the line analysed is the **last one with a score of the main line** (`multipv` 1; `info string ...` is not read), an exact one winning over a `lowerbound` / `upperbound` one (a bound is used only when no exact score came). The score is from the side to move already (UCI), not turned; the depth is the one of that line. `bestmove` with no scored `info` of index 1 before it is `ProtocolError` ("the engine sent no score") **with the process kept** (it is in step). **Several lines (`MultiPV`):** the collector is `UciProtocol::LineCollector(lineCount)`; `Begin` sends `setoption name MultiPV value N` after the `ucinewgame` / `isready` part and before `position`, only when N is not the value the running process has (`_multiPv`: 1 for a new process, set when the option goes out, 1 again when the process is closed), with no `isready` after it (the engine reads its input in order). `FindMove` and a one-line `Analyse` ask for 1, so they send `MultiPV 1` once after a multi-line search and never slow the opponent's own search by a leftover count; a fresh process gets no option for one line, and a replaced process gets it again. Line 1 is the `bestmove` with the score and depth of the index-1 line; each other index is the first move of the `pv` of its last scored line, checked with `MoveGenerator::ParseUci` (not legal: `IllegalMove` naming the line, process kept), a move that is already a line is dropped, as is an index whose line has no `pv` move, so the answer may have fewer lines than asked (Stockfish sends only as many as the position has legal moves; not an error). Scores stay from the side to move; the depth is each line's own. The handshake and the answer to `stop` collect nothing. One search at a time, **`FindMove` and `Analyse` share the mutex**: a game that analyses while the program plays must give the analysis an engine object of its own (a second process), or the program's search waits for it: `PositionAnalyst` does ("The analysis in the game").
- **The opponent in a game** (`EngineOpponent(engine, side, limits)`, any `IChessEngine`: the UCI one, a test double, `OwnEngine` is one, which the person can pick in the window). **Threads:** the main thread calls `Update(model)` once per frame and it never waits for the engine. When it is the opponent's turn, the game is not over, no search is running and no error is waiting, `Update` copies the game (`SearchRequest::FromModel`) and hands `FindMove` to `TaskManager::Run` (the Background pool; a call that blocks for seconds belongs there); a later `Update` finds the answer and plays it with `TryMove`. **The model is read and written only in `Update`, on the main thread**; the worker has a copy of the request and a state of its own (the engine, a `CancellationToken`, the answer behind a mutex, a done flag) held by `eastl::shared_ptr` and passed by value to the coroutine, so nothing dangles whichever side ends first. One search at a time. **A stale answer is dropped:** the model's `GetVersion()` at the time of the request is remembered, and when it has moved (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the running search is cancelled and its answer thrown away, also when the engine ignores the token. A failed search (any `EngineError`; a `Cancelled` of a search the opponent itself cancelled is dropped, not an error, while an engine that returns `Cancelled` without the opponent having cancelled is recorded as one, and no retry loop follows) or a move the model refuses is logged once (`LOG_ERROR`; with `OpponentController` and `PositionAnalyst` the only places in `Engine/` that log), kept in `GetLastError()`, and no search starts until `ClearError()`: a missing engine is one error, not a try per frame. The destructor and `Cancel()` cancel the token and return at once, they never `Wait()`; a search that ignores its token keeps the engine alive until it returns (the engine, and its child process, are then closed on the pool thread). `IsThinking()` is true from the `Update` that starts a search to the one that takes its answer, and false once it is cancelled. One opponent serves one model, and the `TaskManager` must be running. **Shutdown:** destroy the opponent (or `Cancel()` it) before `TaskManager::Shutdown()` and call no `Update()` after it. `Shutdown()` joins the pool, so a running search whose token nobody cancelled holds the join until the engine's own deadline (`movetime` + `answerSlackMs`, 15 s or more by default); a search still queued is run before the join too (the workers drain their queues), with the same wait. An `Update()` after the shutdown starts a search that the scheduler drops (`engine/src/Modules/TaskSystem/CLAUDE.md`): its `done` never rises and `IsThinking()` stays true. The destructor never touches `TaskManager`, so destroying the opponent after the shutdown is safe.
- **The analysis in the game** (`PositionAnalyst`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it; owned by `ChessGame`, which gives it the default factory over `OpponentController::GetSettings()` (read at the time of the search, so the settings `Start()` loads are seen), puts it back to nothing in `Start()` (`Shutdown()`: evaluation off, hint dropped) and shuts it down in `Stop()` next to the opponent, before `TaskManager::Shutdown()`; `ChessBoardComponent::OnUpdate` calls `GetAnalyst().Update(model, GetOpponent().GetEngine())` right after the opponent's update, on the main thread; the control API reads and sets it, see the root `CLAUDE.md`, "Control API"). It is `EngineOpponent`'s pattern for `Analyse`: main thread only, `Update(model, picked)` once per frame, a `Search` state (the engine, a `CancellationToken`, the answer behind a mutex, an atomic `done`) held by `eastl::shared_ptr` and passed by value to a `TaskManager::Run` coroutine on the Background pool, the destructor, `Cancel()` and `Shutdown()` return at once. **One search serves both uses:** the evaluation (`SetEvaluation(bool)`, off by default: it costs a process and CPU) and the hint (`RequestHint(model)`, for the position now on the board); the line is the hint (its move) and the evaluation (its score), as the engine gave it, from the side to move's view (`ScoreForWhite` turns it for a display). Nothing starts while nothing is on or asked, and never for a finished game. **The version rule:** the answer is kept for the model's `GetVersion()` at the request; a running search whose version moved, or whose engine pick changed, is cancelled and its answer dropped, also from an engine that ignores its token; the hint request belongs to its version and goes with the next change of the position (it is never asked again by itself), the evaluation follows the game. **Errors are per version and engine:** a search that fails, an engine the factory cannot make included, is logged once (`LOG_ERROR`) and kept in `GetError()`; no search starts for that version, so a missing Stockfish is one error and not a try per frame; it goes with the next version, a change of the pick, or a new `RequestHint` (the retry). **The engine** is the one the person picked (`OpponentController::GetEngine()`, passed to `Update`), made lazily at the first search by the `AnalysisEngineFactory` (`SetEngineFactory`; the default `MakeAnalysisEngine(engine, settings)`: a `UciEngine` over `FindStockfish(settings.stockfishPath)` with `quitGraceMs = OpponentController::kQuitGraceMs`, or an `OwnEngine`; no `WithHandicap`, no `Skill Level`; `NoStockfishDetail` is the sentence of both this and the opponent's factory), and again after the pick changed. **It is never the opponent's object:** a second process for Stockfish, so the opponent's search never waits for the analysis (the mutex of `UciEngine`). **Nothing falls back to the other engine.** The limits are always those of the strongest level, `LimitsOf(picked, GetOpponentLevels().back())` (Stockfish 2 s, Own depth 5). **The hint rule** is one pure function, `HintRefusalFor(model, programPlays)`: `GameOver` on a finished game, `ProgramsTurn` when the program plays the side to move, nothing for two people; `RequestHint` itself only refuses `GameOver`, the callers (the control adapter, later the HUD) ask `HintRefusalFor` first. The board draws the hint it holds (`GetHint(model)`) on the two squares of its move. `GetRevision()` grows with a switch, a hint asked or dropped, an answer that arrives and an error that appears or goes, never with a search that starts or ends, so a display adds it to the model's version. `Shutdown()` also switches the evaluation off, drops the hint and the engine, so a later `Update` starts nothing; call it before `TaskManager::Shutdown()`. The reads (`GetEvaluation(model)`, `GetHint(model)`) give nothing for a version other than the model's. **The pick is told at once:** `Update` follows the engine pick it is given, but a caller that changes the pick between two frames and reads (the control adapter: `set_state` of `opponent.engine`, then `get_state`) calls `SetPicked(engine)`, which cancels the search of the other engine and drops its engine object, result and error on the spot, so the reads never show the old engine's line for the same position; `ChessGameControl::SetState` does it after a pick and before an analysis request. A change made by the panel is followed by the `Update` of the same frame.
- **The settings file.** Where a person's Stockfish is comes from two config files with one key, the attribute `stockfishPath` of the root element. `config/ChessSettings.xml` is committed and its value is empty (a test reads it from the source tree and fails on a path, so a person's path cannot be committed by mistake); `config/ChessSettings.local.xml` is git-ignored and is where a person puts their own. They are two files with two stems because `ConfigManager` keys a file by its stem and two files with the same stem inside one layer collide (the one scanned later wins), so a local override cannot be `config/local/ChessSettings.xml`: `ChessSettings.local` is a name of its own. The local value wins when it is not empty; a missing file, attribute or value is "not set" and never an error. The lookup order is therefore the config line, then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`: `OpponentSettings::stockfishPath` is the explicit path of `FindStockfish`, an empty one makes it go on to the other two, and a non-empty one that names no file is not skipped (`FindStockfish` has no fallback after a typo). Use an absolute path; a relative one is taken from the working directory of the game. `OpponentSettings::FromConfig(shared, local)` is pure over two `XmlNode` (the name of the root element is not checked, the stem is the key; the values are trimmed), `Load()` reads the two configs from `ConfigManager` and needs the engine services. The game's `config/` is mounted in the executable and not in the gtest environment, so the tests feed `FromConfig` strings. The strength table is `OpponentLevels.*`; only `Skill Level` and the move time are used for Stockfish (`UCI_Elo` has the same floor as Skill 0 and buys nothing; see the layout line), the handicap of `blunderPercent` goes under both engines, and the numbers are hand-tuned guesses that the owner confirms by playing, not measured strengths (the yardsticks of `StockfishGameTest` are small samples). `OpponentController` takes the settings from its caller (`SetSettings`) and uses the table; `ChessGame::Start()` calls `OpponentSettings::Load()` and hands the result to its controller (the tests that start the game through `GameManager` get an empty path: nothing is mounted there, and their engines come from `SetEngineFactory`).
- **The seat of the opponent** (`OpponentController(model, settings)`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it). It holds the choice, which side **the program** plays (`GetPlays()`; none = two people on one screen) which strength (`GetLevel()`, an index of `GetOpponentLevels()`; `kDefaultOpponentLevel`, also while nobody plays) and which engine (`GetEngine()`, an `OpponentEngine`: `Stockfish` or `Own`; `kDefaultOpponentEngine` = `Stockfish`, also while nobody plays), and the `EngineOpponent` behind it. A game starts with nobody: nothing is looked up or started until a side is picked. `SetSide(side or nothing)`, `SetLevel(index)`, `SetEngine(engine)`, `CycleSide()` (nobody -> the program plays Black, so the person has White -> the program plays White -> nobody), `CycleEngine()` (Stockfish -> Own -> Stockfish) and `CycleLevel()` (wraps) destroy the old `EngineOpponent` first (it cancels its search and returns at once; its late answer is never played, also from an engine that ignores its token), ask the engine factory for an engine for `(engine, level)` and make a new `EngineOpponent` with `LimitsOf(engine, level)`: the side is fixed by the `EngineOpponent` constructor and `Skill Level` is sent at the handshake, so a change needs a new engine. While nobody plays, `SetEngine` / `CycleEngine` only change the choice (no factory call, nothing looked up or started) and, like any pick, clear a standing pick error. A pick that changes nothing (the same side, level and engine, no error standing) does nothing, so a search in flight is not thrown away and a running Stockfish is not restarted (this holds for the control API's `set_state` too; with a pick error standing the same side goes through the factory again, which is the retry, and nobody clears it); `Choose(side, level)` (the engine kept) and `Choose(side, level, engine)` are the whole choice in one pick (one engine made, not two or three); when the program takes the side to move, `ChessGameModel::ClearSelection()` drops the person's half-made move. **The factory** (`SetEngineFactory`, an `EngineFactory`: engine and level in, engine or `EngineError` out) is how every test avoids a real Stockfish; the default (an empty factory) makes, for `Stockfish`, a `UciEngine` over `FindStockfish(stockfishPath)` with `options = EngineOptionsOf(level)` and `quitGraceMs = OpponentController::kQuitGraceMs` (500), and for `Own` an `OwnEngine` (in the process, no process, no file looked for, no settings read, it cannot fail), **each wrapped by `WithHandicap(engine, level)`** (a `BlunderEngine` for `easy` and `medium`, the engine itself for `hard` and `max`; a factory given by a test is never wrapped, so the doubles of `TestEngines.h` stay exact). The old engine is dropped on the main thread when no search is running, and `~UciEngine` waits there for a stuck child up to `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s, so a level change blocks the main thread for 1 s at most (the default of `UciEngineSettings` is 5 s; an `OwnEngine` is dropped at once). `SetSettings` and a new factory are read only at the next real pick. **A pick of Stockfish succeeds whenever `FindStockfish` finds a file:** the program is not started until its first search, so a file that is not an engine or not executable shows later, as `LaunchFailed` of that first search (`GetError()`, `opponent.error` on the wire); the game then waits, the program's side stays locked for the person until the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move through the control API) or another pick is made. **A pick that finds no engine is refused** (the factory fails; for the default one `LaunchFailed`, whose detail names the path that was tried and says that `~` and environment variables are not expanded, or the three places when the path is empty; the own engine cannot fail): the engine is asked for FIRST, and when there is none **nothing changes**: the side, the level, the engine and the running `EngineOpponent` with its search stay as they were (an engine that cannot be had cannot be saved; `Choose(side, level, engine)` is all or nothing), the error is kept in `GetError()` (the refused pick's error wins over the opponent's own), the revision grows once and one `LOG_WARNING` names the engine. The old opponent goes on playing while the refused pick's error stands (that error does not stop it); a game that started with nobody stays with two people, so the person is not locked out. A pick that needs no engine (nobody, or `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` while nobody plays) cannot fail. Picking again what already plays, with only a refused pick's error standing, just clears the error (nothing is remade). **No fallback of any kind:** nothing picks the other engine for the person (the owner's decision: the engine is chosen in the window, never silently), so a missing Stockfish stays "Engine not found" until the person picks Own engine in the panel (which clears the error) and then Computer. **The error rule:** an error belongs to the `GetVersion()` of the model it came from (a failed pick, or the opponent's `GetLastError()` noted by the `Update()` that took the answer). When the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the next `Update()` clears the error, the opponent's too (`ClearError()`), and a new pick clears it as well: the person has acted, so they have seen it. Until then no search starts and nothing retries per frame; a broken engine is left by picking nobody (a search that failed; a refused pick leaves the old one playing). `PlaysFor(color)` stays true while an error stands (the side is still the program's). `GetRevision()` grows when the side, the level, the engine or the error changes, never with a search that starts or ends (`IsThinking()`) or a move (the version of the model), so a display watches the three. `Update()` is `EngineOpponent::Update(model)` once per frame, main thread. `Shutdown()` (the destructor too) destroys the opponent: nobody afterwards and `Update()` does nothing. All of it is for the main thread only. **The HUD does not call `CycleSide` / `CycleEngine` / `CycleLevel`** since the panel replaced the cycle buttons: it picks by id through `OpponentChoice` (`SetSide`, `SetEngine`, `SetLevel`). The cycle methods stay on the controller with their `OpponentControllerTest` cases.
- **The choice of the person** (`OpponentChoice(controller)`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it). The controller knows one thing about the sides, the color the PROGRAM plays (`GetPlays()`, none = two people), and so does the control API; the panel speaks of the PERSON: a mode (Two players / Computer) and the color the person plays (the program plays the other one). This class turns between the two views and **remembers the person's color while nobody plays**, when the controller holds no side. `SetMode("computer")` = `SetSide(opposite(Color()))`, `SetMode("two")` = `SetSide(nullopt)` (the color stays), `SetColor(c)` remembers `c` and, when the program plays, `SetSide(opposite(c))` (one new engine), `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` go to the controller as they are; the same pick again is the controller's no-op. The ids are exact (lower case): an empty one, `"White"`, a prefix or one with a trailing space is a `ChoiceError` of the kind `BadId`, nothing changes, no engine is asked and the revision stays; a refused pick is a `ChoiceError` of the kind `Engine` (the controller keeps the error and `Mode()` says Two, the remembered color is the one that was picked). `ChessGame` owns it (after the controller) and `Start()` calls `Reset()`, so a new game starts with White remembered. **`Color()` refreshes the remembered color from the controller:** while the program plays, the person's color is the opposite of `plays`, whoever set it, and that is remembered, so a `set_state` of the control API (`plays: "white"`) is followed by the panel (the person has Black) and `plays: null` afterwards keeps that color. `Color()` is a const getter that writes a mutable member; main thread only. `ChessBoardComponent` reads it on every `SceneUpdateEvent` and on every publication, and `SetMode` reads it before the side goes. **A known gap:** the refresh happens only when `Color()` is read, which is once per frame, so two `set_state` calls with no frame between them (`plays: "white"`, then `plays: null`) leave White remembered; it shows in `opponentColor` and in the side a later Computer pick gives, and on no path of a click.
- **The opponent in the game** (`ChessGame`, `ChessBoardComponent`). `ChessGame` owns the `OpponentController` after the model (`GetOpponent()`) and the `OpponentChoice` right after it (`GetChoice()`), both before the control adapter; `ChessGame::GetActive()`, `GetActiveModel()`, `GetActiveOpponent()` and `GetActiveChoice()` are how components and tests reach them (null when Chess is not the active game). **It is created when a side is picked:** `Start()` resets the model, loads the settings and sets nobody, the default level and the default engine (Stockfish), so a game with no engine configured plays as two people on one screen and nothing is looked up or started; an engine is made by the first `SetSide()` on `GetOpponent()` (what `SetMode("computer")` of the choice does); the board component calls `SetMode` / `SetColor` / `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` of the choice for the actions `ChessSetMode` / `ChessSetColor` / `ChessSetEngine` / `ChessSetLevel` with the `value` of the event (an id) and shows and hides the panel for `ChessOpenOpponent` / `ChessCloseOpponent`; the segments of the panel and the `Menu` and OK buttons emit them. **The frame:** `IGame` has no per-frame hook, so `ChessBoardComponent` subscribes to `SceneEvents::SceneUpdateEvent` (`SubscribeInScene`) and calls `Update()` there, on the main thread; never in `OnDraw()`, which must not change the model. The program's move is played in that update. **The click guard:** `ChessBoardComponent::OnMouseDown` returns, after the "game over: a click starts the next game" branch, when `PlaysFor(model->GetTurn())`: `OnSquareClicked` does not know sides, and a person who selected the program's piece would only fight the program's own search. The guard holds while a search error stands too (`PlaysFor` stays true), so the person's ways out matter and are tested: the Reset button (the version moves, the error rule clears it), a pick in the panel (a color, engine or level: a new opponent), and picking Two players. The control API is not blocked for the program's side. **A new game needs nothing special:** the Reset button, a click after the end, the control `reset` and `set_state` move `GetVersion()`, which cancels the running search and drops its answer, and the program, when it plays the side to move, opens the new game. **Shutdown:** `ChessGame::Stop()` calls the controller's `Shutdown()`. `Application::Cleanup()` runs `GameManager::Shutdown()` (which calls `Stop()` and then destroys the games) before `CoreManager::OnApplicationDeinit()` shuts the task system down, so no search holds the join of the pool; `GameManager` calls `Stop()` when it switches away from a game as well. A test shuts the game down with a gated search that ignores its token, and it returns at once. The result of a game is logged once by whoever sees it first (`LogResultOnce`: the click that ended it, or the update after the move of the program or of the control API). The words and ids of the HUD and the panel (`opponentMode`, `opponentColor`, `opponentEngine`, `opponentLevel`, `opponentError`, "Computer is thinking", the error sentence) are published and the panel has the groups that pick the mode, the color, the engine and the level. **The control API** reports and sets the opponent (below). **The engine of our own** (`OwnEngine`, above) is the second engine, picked by the person in the Engine row of the panel (or `opponent.engine` of the control API): its levels are a depth each (`OpponentLevel::ownDepth`), it runs in the process on every target (Windows and the web included) and needs no install. **The default is Stockfish and nothing falls back:** a game with no Stockfish reports "Engine not found" until the person picks Own engine in the panel; a product choice for the owner is making `Own` the default (a one-line change of `kDefaultOpponentEngine`, which would still not be a fallback).
