# Chess Module Guidelines

## Overview

`ChessGame` is an `IGame` (name `Chess`, scene `ChessScene`). Executable `BagiChess`. Infrastructure and how a game is registered: `engine/src/Modules/Games/CLAUDE.md`. Namespace `BECore::Chess`. The game's content is in `config/` (`ApplicationConfig.xml`, `SceneConfig.xml`, `TextureLibrary.xml`, `ChessSettings.xml`) and `assets/`. `BagiChess` starts in Game mode (`mode="Game"` in its `ApplicationConfig.xml`, no editor); set `mode="Editor"` to open it in the scene editor.

## Layout

```
src/Chess/
├── ChessGame.{h,cpp}         IGame; owns the shared ChessGameModel, the OpponentController and the control adapter; Start() resets the model, loads OpponentSettings and starts with nobody as the opponent, Reset() resets the model, Stop() destroys the opponent; GetActive() / GetActiveModel() / GetActiveOpponent() are how components reach them
├── ChessGameControl.{h,cpp}  IControllableGame: the state, the legal moves, a way to play one, to load a FEN and to pick the computer opponent for the control API (below); holds the model and the `OpponentController`
├── Game/                     plain C++ rules, no scene or renderer
│   ├── ChessTypes.h          Square (file, rank), PieceType, PieceColor, Piece
│   ├── Bitboard.h            Bitboard (a1 = bit 0), masks, shifts, PopCount / Lsb / PopLsb
│   ├── Move.h                Move: 16 bits (from, to, promotion piece, kind), ToUci()
│   ├── Attacks.*             AttackTables (SingletonAtomic): knight / king / pawn tables, rook and bishop magic bitboards, Between / Line, Zobrist keys
│   ├── Position.*            pieces as bitboards + 64-cell array, side to move, castling rights, en passant, clocks, Zobrist key; FEN, DoMove / UndoMove, IsLegal, checkers and pins
│   ├── MoveGenerator.*       GenerateLegal(position) -> MoveList; ParseUci(position, text)
│   ├── GameStatus.*          EvaluateStatus(position): Ongoing, Checkmate, Stalemate, DrawInsufficientMaterial, DrawThreefold, DrawFiftyMoves
│   ├── StatusText.*          the words of the HUD: Turn(model, opponentThinking = false) ("White to move", "White wins", "Draw"; "Computer is thinking" while the game is on and the program searches) and Status(model) ("", "Check", "Checkmate", "Draw: threefold repetition", ...)
│   └── ChessGameModel.*      selected square, legal moves of it, status, move history, version, piece handles; OnSquareClicked drives select / move, TryMove(Move) is the one place a move is applied, SetPosition(Position) starts a game from a FEN, ClearSelection() drops a half-made move for an owner that takes the turn
├── Engine/                   the opponent, a program that picks a move (ChessGame owns its controller, ChessBoardComponent runs it; nothing in the window picks a side yet); depends on Game/ and on nothing in Components/
│   ├── ChessEngine.h         IChessEngine::FindMove(request, token): the interface; EngineError (a kind from EngineErrorKind and a sentence), SearchLimits (move time, depth)
│   ├── SearchRequest.*       SearchRequest: start FEN + moves + limits, from a model (FromModel) or from a position (FromPosition); Validate() the limits, FinalPosition() the position the moves lead to
│   ├── UciProtocol.*         the UCI text, pure: PositionCommand, GoCommand, SetOptionCommand, ParseBestMove, TrimLine
│   ├── UciProcess.*          a child process talked to in lines: Start(ProcessLaunch), WriteLine, ReadLine(timeout), Close(grace); the transport under a UCI engine, no UCI in it. POSIX only
│   ├── UciEngine.*           UciEngine: IChessEngine over UciProcess (handshake, ucinewgame, position / go / bestmove, timeouts, cancellation, a new process after an error); UciEngineSettings; FindStockfish(path): the explicit path, $STOCKFISH, stockfish on PATH
│   ├── EngineOpponent.*      EngineOpponent: plays one side of a ChessGameModel against any IChessEngine; Update(model) on the main thread starts the search on the Background pool and plays the answer with TryMove; IsThinking, Cancel, GetLastError / ClearError
│   ├── OpponentSettings.*    OpponentSettings: the path of Stockfish from `config/ChessSettings.xml` and the git-ignored `config/ChessSettings.local.xml` (FromConfig over two XmlNodes, Load() from ConfigManager; `ChessGame::Start()` calls it)
│   ├── OpponentLevels.*      the strengths of the opponent: easy, medium, hard, max (id, name, `Skill Level`, move time); FindOpponentLevel(id), LimitsOf(level) -> SearchLimits, EngineOptionsOf(level) -> UciEngineSettings::options
│   ├── OpponentText.*        the words of the HUD for the opponent, pure (`Game/` must not know the engine, so they are not in `StatusText`): SideLabel(computerPlays) ("Two players", "You: White", "You: Black"), LevelLabel(level) ("Level: Medium"), Error(EngineError) (a sentence of at most `kMaxErrorLength` = 28 characters per `EngineErrorKind`, a `switch` without `default`: "Engine not found", "Engine did not answer", ...)
│   └── OpponentController.*  OpponentController: the seat of the opponent for one ChessGameModel; SetSide / SetLevel / Choose (both in one pick) / CycleSide / CycleLevel remake the EngineOpponent behind the choice, Update() per frame, PlaysFor(color), IsThinking, GetError, GetRevision, Shutdown; EngineFactory makes the engine (default: Stockfish of OpponentSettings)
└── Components/               scene glue (IComponent)
    ├── ChessBoardComponent   draws the board, the check and the highlights, turns mouse clicks into OnSquareClicked (and restarts after the game ends; ignores a click while the program plays the side to move), runs the opponent's Update() on every SceneUpdateEvent and logs the result of a game once, publishes `turn`, `status`, `side` and `level` to UIVars when the model, the opponent's revision or its search changed and shows the HUD element `Level` only while the program plays, answers the actions ChessReset, ChessCycleSide and ChessCycleLevel
    ├── ChessPieceComponent   logical handle of one piece; drives its TransformComponent from the model and switches its sprite after a promotion
    └── ChessResultComponent  on its own node (layer 1, over the pieces): dims the board and draws the result bar once the game is over
```

- State lives in `ChessGameModel`. Components never keep game state: they reach the model through `ChessGame::GetActiveModel()` (null when Chess is not the active game, and then they do nothing).
- A piece node in `ChessScene` is a `ChessPieceComponent` + `SpriteRendererComponent` (artwork from `TextureLibrary`, `config/TextureLibrary.xml`) + `TransformComponent`. `OnAttached` registers the piece with the model at its starting square (a piece that registers late, in the first frame, after a client has already moved, is dealt to the position as it stands: `RegisterPiece` re-deals when the game has left the opening); `Reset()` sends every handle back. The model moves a handle with its piece through captures, castling (the rook too), en passant (the passed pawn's handle is the one removed) and promotion (the handle reports the new type and the component switches the sprite to `<Color><Type>`, e.g. `WhiteQueen`).
- **The node is the whole square, the sprite is fitted inside it.** `ChessPieceComponent` writes the node's position and `_width = _height = squareSize` every update, and the sprite of each of the 32 piece nodes is `fit="Contain"` (`SpriteFit`, `engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`): the piece is drawn inside the square with the aspect of its atlas cell (116.67 x 190.5, tall), as high as the square and centered. The board fills the selection, legal-move and check highlights over the same full square, so they sit around the piece and `ChessBoardComponent` does not need to know the size of the piece. A new piece node needs the attribute (`Stretch`, the default, squashes the tall cell into the square; a test counts the 32).
- The piece atlas (`assets/chess_test.png`) must keep a real alpha channel: a sprite with an opaque background paints a box over its square and hides the square color and the highlights. A replacement atlas needs a transparent background. Its cells may have any aspect: the fit keeps whatever `srcRect` the library gives, so the cell size in `TextureLibrary.xml` and the art can change without touching the scene.
- Board geometry (`_originX`, `_originY`, `_squareSize`) is reflected so it can be tuned in the scene editor. It is in scene units, not window pixels: `ChessScene` declares `viewWidth="840" viewHeight="800"` (the board 640x640 at (100, 80): 100 units on the sides and 80 above and below, where the HUD sits), so the engine scales the whole scene to the window and centers it. Change the view size together with the board geometry, and map mouse positions with `Scene::ScreenToSceneX/Y` (`OnMouseDown` does).
- **The HUD** is a `SceneView` canvas on the `Hud` node (layer 10, over the end-of-game dim), in the two margins of the view, **one row of 56-unit controls in each**, 12 units from the edge of the safe area, every control anchored to a corner. Top row: `Turn` (label `{turn}`, left, 320 wide, font 28), `Level` (button `{level}`, action `ChessCycleLevel`, 190 wide, font 24, blue) and `Reset` (button, `ChessReset`, 110 wide, font 26, green). Bottom row: `Side` (button `{side}`, `ChessCycleSide`, left, 170 wide, font 24, brown) and `Status` (label `{status}`, bottom right, `60%` wide, right aligned, font 28). The two new buttons have fills of their own so they do not read as Reset (and so a test finds each where the screen draws it). It scales and moves with the board. That it stays outside the board is checked in five window shapes without a safe-area inset (`TheHudStaysOutsideTheBoardWithoutInsets`); with a notch or a home-bar inset in a landscape or square window a row can reach onto the edge of the board (the old HUD did too). **Why one row per margin:** the margin is 80 units, a 56-unit button is a 32 px touch target at the smallest fit scale of the size matrix (0.57, because the view is 840 units wide) and the old 60-unit margin left no room; a second row would need a taller view and a smaller board. **The width budget** is set by the narrowest case of the matrix, a 480 px wide window with side insets (9 % each side): scale 0.571, a safe area of about 689 units. Top row: `Turn` ends at 332, `Level` spans about 365 to 555 and `Reset` 567 to 677 (gaps of 33 and 12); "Computer is thinking" needs 249 of the 320. Bottom row: `Side` ends at 182, `Status` is 413 wide and starts at about 263; the widest sentence ("Engine made an illegal move") needs 347, "Draw: insufficient material" 317, and the widest button text ("Level: Medium") 151 of 190. A wider window only adds room (the anchors are corners and `Status` is a percent), so the narrowest case decides. **The side and the strength are cycle buttons** because the engine has no toggle, segmented control or dropdown (engine entries `in-game-ui`, `ui-authoring`): each press goes to the next choice and the text says the current one ("Two players" -> "You: White" -> "You: Black"; "Level: Medium" -> Hard -> Max -> Easy). A real selector replaces them when the engine has one. `Level` is hidden by the board while nobody is the opponent (an empty rect: it draws nothing and takes no click), because the strength means something only when the program plays. **Thinking and errors:** "Computer is thinking" is the `Turn` label and "Black to move" comes back when the answer is played; the engine's error is the `Status` label and wins over "Check" while it lasts. While an error stands `Turn` still says whose move it is ("Black to move") and only `Status` explains; the sentence says what went wrong and never what to do next (the README section "Playing against the computer" does: Reset, "Two players", the path). The window never freezes without a word: a search says so, a failure says so, and Reset, a side, a level or a move of someone else lets the person out. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` runs the HUD through the size matrix with every word the game can show, in both states of the opponent (`Level` hidden, then shown), and the words come from the tables through `tests/HudWords.h` (`StatusText` over a scratch game, `OpponentText`, `GetOpponentLevels()`, every `EngineErrorKind`), never a copy: the font is proportional, so the longest word by characters is not always the widest and each word gets a round. The validator's `TextDoesNotFit` does not see a label that cuts its text with an ellipsis (a label with `Ellipsis` "can shrink"), so the test asks `CheckContent().fits` of every shown control itself (a button must also keep 8 units of air between its text and the edge of its fill) and demands 8 units between any two controls. `ChessHudWebTest` holds the same five elements written in HTML + CSS to the same rects. The promotion picker waits for the engine entry `chess-staged-stream`.
- **The words of the HUD** are four `UIVars` that `ChessBoardComponent` publishes in `OnDraw()` (the UI tests read the labels after a `Draw()` with no update), again whenever the model's version, the controller's `GetRevision()` or its `IsThinking()` changed (a search starts and ends without a move of the game, a side or a level changes without one): `turn` ("White to move", "Computer is thinking" while the program searches and the game is on, "White wins" / "Draw" once it is over: `StatusText::Turn(model, thinking)`), `status` (the sentence of the opponent's error while one stands, `OpponentText::Error`, else the status of the game; the error wins over "Check" because the game is stalled by it and the person has to read it; any act clears it: Reset, a side, a level or a move of someone else; the detail with the paths goes to the log and the control API), `side` (`OpponentText::SideLabel`: "Two players", "You: White" when the program plays Black, "You: Black" when it plays White; a pick that found no engine leaves "Two players" and puts the error into `status`) and `level` ("Level: Medium"). The board component answers two more actions: `ChessCycleSide` (`OpponentController::CycleSide()`) and `ChessCycleLevel` (`CycleLevel()`); a failed pick needs no handling there, it is shown through `GetError()`. The element named `Level` of any canvas of the scene is shown only while the program plays (`UICanvasComponent::SetElementVisibleByName`, on the first publication and whenever the revision changed); a scene without such an element is fine. The buttons that emit the two actions are in the HUD above (`Side`, `Level`).
- The `Board` node has `layer="-1"`, the pieces stay on layer 0 and the `Result` node has `layer="1"`, so the squares and highlights are drawn first, the sprites on top of them and the end-of-game dim over everything. The order comes from the layer, not from where the node sits in `SceneConfig.xml` (`engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`, "Draw order").
- **The opponent** (`Engine/`). `IChessEngine::FindMove` is synchronous and blocking: it returns when the engine has answered, was cancelled (`CancellationToken`) or failed, so a caller that must not wait runs it on a worker. It takes a `SearchRequest` (the start FEN of the game and the moves played, plus the limits), not a `Position`: UCI engines see repetitions only through `position fen <startFen> moves ...`, which a bare `Position` cannot give (`SearchRequest::FromPosition` drops the history, `FromModel` keeps it). Errors are values, `std::expected<Move, EngineError>`; a move that comes back is legal in the position the request leads to. `UciProtocol` writes the same `position` command as `position_command` of `tools/stockfish_bridge.py`. `UciProcess` is the child process under it (POSIX; Windows answers `Unsupported`): the child gets pipes for stdin and stdout, `/dev/null` for stderr and no other descriptor (the control socket of the game stays out of it; this holds on macOS and with glibc 2.34 or later, on older glibc and musl a descriptor of the game without close-on-exec is inherited, and Linux is not built or tested here), and a write to a dead child is `ProcessDied`, not `SIGPIPE` (on macOS the pipe carries `F_SETNOSIGPIPE`; elsewhere `Start()` sets `SIGPIPE` to ignored for the whole process, which the header says).
- **The UCI engine** (`UciEngine`) is `IChessEngine` over `UciProcess`, the port of `UciEngine` of the bridge. **The path comes from the caller** (`UciEngineSettings::launch`); `FindStockfish(path)` only looks for one, like `find_stockfish` of the bridge: the explicit path (it must be a file; when it is not there is no fallback), then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`. Nothing here knows where Stockfish is installed and nothing of it is shipped. Stockfish is GPL-3.0: it is neither linked nor copied, none of its code or files enter this repository or `engine/`, it is a separate program the user installs and the game starts through a path. The constructor starts nothing; the process starts on the first `FindMove` and again on the first one after an error closed it. One search at a time (a mutex); the waits go in slices of 20 ms, so a cancelled `CancellationToken` is seen soon. Failures are `EngineError` kinds: `BadRequest` (limits or request wrong; no process is started), `NoMove` (the game is over, which the engine is not asked, or it answered `bestmove (none)` / `0000`), `IllegalMove` (a move the position does not allow, its text in `detail`), `LaunchFailed`, `Timeout` (no `uciok` / `readyok` within `startupTimeoutMs`, no `bestmove` within `movetime + answerSlackMs`, or `depthOnlyTimeoutMs` for a depth without time), `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError`, `Cancelled` (`stop` is sent and the answer to it is read and dropped), `Unsupported` (Windows). **What closes the process** (`quit`, `quitGraceMs`, then `SIGTERM` and `SIGKILL`; always reaped): `Timeout`, `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError`, a cancelled search whose engine does not answer `stop` within `stopGraceMs`, a cancel during the setup (`uciok` / `readyok`, `ucinewgame` + `isready`: no search runs yet, so no `stop` is sent, the process is closed and `Cancelled` comes back once the process has gone: at once for an engine that obeys `quit`, after the `quitGraceMs` and the signals for one that does not), and the destructor (on the thread that destroys the engine, up to the `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s for the signals). `NoMove`, `IllegalMove` and a cancelled search the engine did answer keep it. `ucinewgame` goes with the first search of a process, another start FEN, or moves that do not extend the previous request's.
- **The opponent in a game** (`EngineOpponent(engine, side, limits)`, any `IChessEngine`: the UCI one, a test double, the own engine of a later slice). **Threads:** the main thread calls `Update(model)` once per frame and it never waits for the engine. When it is the opponent's turn, the game is not over, no search is running and no error is waiting, `Update` copies the game (`SearchRequest::FromModel`) and hands `FindMove` to `TaskManager::Run` (the Background pool; a call that blocks for seconds belongs there); a later `Update` finds the answer and plays it with `TryMove`. **The model is read and written only in `Update`, on the main thread**; the worker has a copy of the request and a state of its own (the engine, a `CancellationToken`, the answer behind a mutex, a done flag) held by `eastl::shared_ptr` and passed by value to the coroutine, so nothing dangles whichever side ends first. One search at a time. **A stale answer is dropped:** the model's `GetVersion()` at the time of the request is remembered, and when it has moved (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the running search is cancelled and its answer thrown away, also when the engine ignores the token. A failed search (any `EngineError`; a `Cancelled` of a search the opponent itself cancelled is dropped, not an error, while an engine that returns `Cancelled` without the opponent having cancelled is recorded as one, and no retry loop follows) or a move the model refuses is logged once (`LOG_ERROR`; with `OpponentController` the only places in `Engine/` that log), kept in `GetLastError()`, and no search starts until `ClearError()`: a missing engine is one error, not a try per frame. The destructor and `Cancel()` cancel the token and return at once, they never `Wait()`; a search that ignores its token keeps the engine alive until it returns (the engine, and its child process, are then closed on the pool thread). `IsThinking()` is true from the `Update` that starts a search to the one that takes its answer, and false once it is cancelled. One opponent serves one model, and the `TaskManager` must be running. **Shutdown:** destroy the opponent (or `Cancel()` it) before `TaskManager::Shutdown()` and call no `Update()` after it. `Shutdown()` joins the pool, so a running search whose token nobody cancelled holds the join until the engine's own deadline (`movetime` + `answerSlackMs`, 15 s or more by default); a search still queued is run before the join too (the workers drain their queues), with the same wait. An `Update()` after the shutdown starts a search that the scheduler drops (`engine/src/Modules/TaskSystem/CLAUDE.md`): its `done` never rises and `IsThinking()` stays true. The destructor never touches `TaskManager`, so destroying the opponent after the shutdown is safe.
- **The settings file.** Where a person's Stockfish is comes from two config files with one key, the attribute `stockfishPath` of the root element. `config/ChessSettings.xml` is committed and its value is empty (a test reads it from the source tree and fails on a path, so a person's path cannot be committed by mistake); `config/ChessSettings.local.xml` is git-ignored and is where a person puts their own. They are two files with two stems because `ConfigManager` keys a file by its stem and two files with the same stem inside one layer collide (the one scanned later wins), so a local override cannot be `config/local/ChessSettings.xml`: `ChessSettings.local` is a name of its own. The local value wins when it is not empty; a missing file, attribute or value is "not set" and never an error. The lookup order is therefore the config line, then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`: `OpponentSettings::stockfishPath` is the explicit path of `FindStockfish`, an empty one makes it go on to the other two, and a non-empty one that names no file is not skipped (`FindStockfish` has no fallback after a typo). Use an absolute path; a relative one is taken from the working directory of the game. `OpponentSettings::FromConfig(shared, local)` is pure over two `XmlNode` (the name of the root element is not checked, the stem is the key; the values are trimmed), `Load()` reads the two configs from `ConfigManager` and needs the engine services. The game's `config/` is mounted in the executable and not in the gtest environment, so the tests feed `FromConfig` strings. The strength table is `OpponentLevels.*`; only `Skill Level` and the move time are used (`UCI_Elo` exists only in newer Stockfish), and the numbers are first guesses to tune by hand against a real Stockfish. `OpponentController` takes the settings from its caller (`SetSettings`) and uses the table; `ChessGame::Start()` calls `OpponentSettings::Load()` and hands the result to its controller (the tests that start the game through `GameManager` get an empty path: nothing is mounted there, and their engines come from `SetEngineFactory`).
- **The seat of the opponent** (`OpponentController(model, settings)`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it). It holds the choice, which side **the program** plays (`GetPlays()`; none = two people on one screen) and which strength (`GetLevel()`, an index of `GetOpponentLevels()`; `kDefaultOpponentLevel`, also while nobody plays), and the `EngineOpponent` behind it. A game starts with nobody: nothing is looked up or started until a side is picked. `SetSide(side or nothing)`, `SetLevel(index)`, `CycleSide()` (nobody -> the program plays Black, so the person has White -> the program plays White -> nobody) and `CycleLevel()` (wraps) destroy the old `EngineOpponent` first (it cancels its search and returns at once; its late answer is never played, also from an engine that ignores its token), ask the engine factory for an engine and make a new `EngineOpponent` with `LimitsOf(level)`: the side is fixed by the `EngineOpponent` constructor and `Skill Level` is sent at the handshake, so a change needs a new engine. A pick that changes nothing (the same side and level, no error standing) does nothing, so a search in flight is not thrown away and a running Stockfish is not restarted (this holds for the control API's `set_state` too; with a pick error standing the same side goes through the factory again, which is the retry, and nobody clears it); `Choose(side, level)` is both in one pick (one engine made, not two); when the program takes the side to move, `ChessGameModel::ClearSelection()` drops the person's half-made move. **The factory** (`SetEngineFactory`, an `EngineFactory`: level in, engine or `EngineError` out) is how every test avoids a real Stockfish; the default (an empty factory) is a `UciEngine` over `FindStockfish(stockfishPath)` with `options = EngineOptionsOf(level)` and `quitGraceMs = OpponentController::kQuitGraceMs` (500): the old engine is dropped on the main thread when no search is running, and `~UciEngine` waits there for a stuck child up to `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s, so a level change blocks the main thread for 1 s at most (the default of `UciEngineSettings` is 5 s). `SetSettings` and a new factory are read only at the next real pick. **A pick succeeds whenever `FindStockfish` finds a file:** the program is not started until its first search, so a file that is not an engine or not executable shows later, as `LaunchFailed` of that first search (`GetError()`, `opponent.error` on the wire); the game then waits, the program's side stays locked for the person until the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move through the control API) or another pick is made. **A pick that finds no engine** (the factory fails; for the default one `LaunchFailed`, whose detail names the path that was tried and says that `~` and environment variables are not expanded, or the three places when the path is empty) leaves nobody as the opponent, keeps the error in `GetError()`, and logs one `LOG_WARNING`; the person is not locked out, both colors can still be moved by hand. **The error rule:** an error belongs to the `GetVersion()` of the model it came from (a failed pick, or the opponent's `GetLastError()` noted by the `Update()` that took the answer). When the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the next `Update()` clears the error, the opponent's too (`ClearError()`), and a new pick clears it as well: the person has acted, so they have seen it. Until then no search starts and nothing retries per frame; a broken engine is left by picking nobody. `PlaysFor(color)` stays true while an error stands (the side is still the program's). `GetRevision()` grows when the side, the level or the error changes, never with a search that starts or ends (`IsThinking()`) or a move (the version of the model), so a display watches the three. `Update()` is `EngineOpponent::Update(model)` once per frame, main thread. `Shutdown()` (the destructor too) destroys the opponent: nobody afterwards and `Update()` does nothing. All of it is for the main thread only.
- **The opponent in the game** (`ChessGame`, `ChessBoardComponent`). `ChessGame` owns the `OpponentController` after the model and before the control adapter (`GetOpponent()`); `ChessGame::GetActive()`, `GetActiveModel()` and `GetActiveOpponent()` are how components and tests reach them (null when Chess is not the active game). **It is created when a side is picked:** `Start()` resets the model, loads the settings and sets nobody and the default level, so a game with no engine configured plays as two people on one screen and nothing is looked up or started; an engine is made by the first `SetSide()` / `CycleSide()` on `GetOpponent()`; the board component calls `CycleSide()` / `CycleLevel()` for the actions `ChessCycleSide` / `ChessCycleLevel`, and the `Side` and `Level` buttons of the HUD emit them. **The frame:** `IGame` has no per-frame hook, so `ChessBoardComponent` subscribes to `SceneEvents::SceneUpdateEvent` (`SubscribeInScene`) and calls `Update()` there, on the main thread; never in `OnDraw()`, which must not change the model. The program's move is played in that update. **The click guard:** `ChessBoardComponent::OnMouseDown` returns, after the "game over: a click starts the next game" branch, when `PlaysFor(model->GetTurn())`: `OnSquareClicked` does not know sides, and a person who selected the program's piece would only fight the program's own search. The guard holds while a search error stands too (`PlaysFor` stays true), so the person's ways out matter and are tested: the Reset button (the version moves, the error rule clears it), a change of side or level (a new opponent), and picking nobody. The control API is not blocked for the program's side. **A new game needs nothing special:** the Reset button, a click after the end, the control `reset` and `set_state` move `GetVersion()`, which cancels the running search and drops its answer, and the program, when it plays the side to move, opens the new game. **Shutdown:** `ChessGame::Stop()` calls the controller's `Shutdown()`. `Application::Cleanup()` runs `GameManager::Shutdown()` (which calls `Stop()` and then destroys the games) before `CoreManager::OnApplicationDeinit()` shuts the task system down, so no search holds the join of the pool; `GameManager` calls `Stop()` when it switches away from a game as well. A test shuts the game down with a gated search that ignores its token, and it returns at once. The result of a game is logged once by whoever sees it first (`LogResultOnce`: the click that ended it, or the update after the move of the program or of the control API). The words of the HUD (`side`, `level`, "Computer is thinking", the error sentence) are published and the HUD has the buttons that pick the side and the level. **The control API** reports and sets the opponent (below). Deferred, with the reason: the board does not flip when the person plays Black and the promotion has no picker (each is a slice of its own); the choice of side and level is not remembered between runs (nothing asked for it); Windows (`UciProcess` is POSIX only).

## Control API

`ChessGame::AsControllable()` hands out `ChessGameControl` (`engine/src/Modules/Games/CLAUDE.md` for the methods and the rules of an adapter). The model and the opponent controller belong to the game, so the adapter holds references and has no `no_active_model`. The wire format is in the header comment of `ChessGameControl.h`; in short:

| Method | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| `get_state` | `{fen, board, turn, status, winner, inCheck, legalMoveCount, startFen, moves, opponent}`: `board` is 8 strings, rank 8 first, white upper case, `.` empty; `status` is `ongoing`, `checkmate`, `stalemate`, `draw_insufficient_material`, `draw_threefold` or `draw_fifty_moves`; `startFen` plus `moves` (UCI) rebuild the game with its repetitions; `opponent` is `{plays, level, thinking, error}`: `plays` the color the **program** plays (`"white"`, `"black"`, or `null` for two people, the start of every game), `level` the id of the strength (`easy`, `medium`, `hard`, `max`; the one that would be used, also while `plays` is null), `thinking` whether a search runs, `error` `null` or `{kind, detail}` (`kind` in snake_case: `launch_failed`, `timeout`, `process_died`, `protocol_error`, `illegal_move`, `no_move`, `bad_request`, `cancelled`, `unsupported`; `detail` the sentence of the log). A pick succeeds whenever a Stockfish file is found, so a launch that fails shows only at the first search, as `error` `launch_failed` |
| `list_actions` | `{"type": "move", "uci": "e2e4"}` for every legal move (a promotion is four); empty once the game is over |
| `perform_action` | plays one of them and answers `{move, status, turn, winner, inCheck}`; errors `illegal_action`, `promotion_required` (`e7e8` without the piece), `game_over`, `invalid_params` (not a UCI move) |
| `reset` | the opening position; takes no options; **keeps the opponent** (side and level; a standing error goes by the version rule) |
| `set_state` | `{"fen": ".."}` and/or `{"opponent": {"plays": "white"\|"black"\|null, "level": "easy"\|"medium"\|"hard"\|"max"}}` (inside `opponent` a missing member means unchanged; `{}` changes nothing). A FEN starts a game from any position `FromFen` accepts, finished ones included (the pieces in the scene are dealt out again, `SetPosition`). Neither of the two, a side that is not `white` / `black` / null, an unknown level, a member of the wrong type: `invalid_params`, and nothing is changed (everything is checked first). The opponent is applied first, then the position. No engine for the pick (no Stockfish found): `engine_unavailable` with the engine's sentence; the position is **not** replaced, the program plays nobody afterwards (the level stays picked) and `opponent.error` has the reason. A pick that changes nothing does nothing (no restart, no new version); `plays: null` clears an error |

- The state version is `ChessGameModel::GetVersion()` plus `OpponentController::GetRevision()`. It grows on a played move, a reset, a `set_state`, a pick of the side or level and an error that appears or goes, not on a selection or on a search that starts or ends (`thinking`). A person's move by mouse and the program's move grow it too, so `state_changed` reports them. It never goes back.
- `list_actions` is unchanged by the opponent: every action is a move, which `tools/stockfish_bridge.py` relies on. `perform_action` is not blocked for the program's side: a controller may play for it (a search that this makes stale is dropped by the version); only the mouse is kept from the program's side.
- The scene follows the model: a move, a promotion, a reset and a `set_state` all show in a `screenshot`, which is how a headless run is checked by eye.

## Rules

Standard chess, Chess960 excluded: legal moves only (pins, check, castling, en passant, promotion), checkmate, stalemate and the draws that end the game by themselves: insufficient material, threefold repetition and 50 moves without a capture or a pawn move. FIDE makes a player claim the last two (it ends a game by itself only at fivefold and 75 moves), but the window has no way to claim, so they apply automatically. Both thresholds are named constants in `GameStatus.cpp`.

- A move is a `Move` from `MoveGenerator::GenerateLegal()`; `ChessGameModel::TryMove()` accepts nothing else and answers `std::expected<void, MoveError>` (`GameOver`, `IllegalMove`). `Position::IsLegal()` assumes a pseudo-legal move from the generator (while in check it relies on the generator having restricted the targets), so do not call it with a hand-made move: parse text with `MoveGenerator::ParseUci()`.
- Castling is the king's move (`e1g1`), as in UCI. A promoting pawn has four moves per target, queen first; a click on the target plays the queen, the other pieces are available through `TryMove` / UCI (`e7e8n`) only.
- `Position` keeps the en passant square only when a pawn can capture onto it (so positions that are equal for the rules have equal keys and FEN). `FromFen` rejects positions the rules cannot reach in the ways that would break the lookups: a king count other than one, a pawn on a back rank, more than 16 pieces of one color (the scene has 16 piece nodes per color, so any accepted position can be shown), the side that just moved in check, castling rights without the king and rook in place.
- A finished game accepts no move. The window shows the end with the red king (bright on check, dark on mate), the dimmed board and a bar across it (white, black or gray for a draw); the HUD says who won and why ("White wins", "Checkmate"), and the reason also goes to the log. A click on the board after the end, or the Reset button, starts a new game.
- The magic bitboards are searched for on first use (about 90 ms in a Debug build, measured on macOS) with a fixed seed, every candidate checked against the slow ray walk. If that becomes too slow, print the found numbers once and embed them as constants.

New rule code goes into `Game/` with a gtest case; do not patch around the generator in the model or the components.
