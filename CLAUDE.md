# Chess Module Guidelines

## Overview

`ChessGame` is an `IGame` (name `Chess`, scene `ChessScene`). Executable `BagiChess`. Infrastructure and how a game is registered: `engine/src/Modules/Games/CLAUDE.md`. Namespace `BECore::Chess`. The game's content is in `config/` (`ApplicationConfig.xml`, `SceneConfig.xml`, `TextureLibrary.xml`, `ChessSettings.xml`) and `assets/`. `BagiChess` starts in Game mode (`mode="Game"` in its `ApplicationConfig.xml`, no editor); set `mode="Editor"` to open it in the scene editor.

## Layout

```
src/Chess/
├── ChessGame.{h,cpp}         IGame; owns the shared ChessGameModel, the OpponentController, the OpponentChoice over it and the control adapter; Start() resets the model, loads OpponentSettings, starts with nobody as the opponent and Stockfish as the engine and forgets the person's color (White), Reset() resets the model, Stop() destroys the opponent; GetActive() / GetActiveModel() / GetActiveOpponent() / GetActiveChoice() are how components reach them
├── ChessGameControl.{h,cpp}  IControllableGame: the state, the legal moves, a way to play one, to load a FEN and to pick the computer opponent for the control API (below); holds the model and the `OpponentController`
├── Game/                     plain C++ rules, no scene or renderer
│   ├── ChessTypes.h          Square (file, rank), PieceType, PieceColor, Piece
│   ├── Bitboard.h            Bitboard (a1 = bit 0), masks, shifts, PopCount / Lsb / PopLsb
│   ├── Move.h                Move: 16 bits (from, to, promotion piece, kind), ToUci()
│   ├── Attacks.*             AttackTables (SingletonAtomic): knight / king / pawn tables, rook and bishop magic bitboards, Between / Line, Zobrist keys
│   ├── Position.*            pieces as bitboards + 64-cell array, side to move, castling rights, en passant, clocks, Zobrist key; FEN, DoMove / UndoMove, IsLegal, checkers and pins
│   ├── MoveGenerator.*       GenerateLegal(position) -> MoveList; ParseUci(position, text)
│   ├── GameStatus.*          EvaluateStatus(position): Ongoing, Checkmate, Stalemate, DrawInsufficientMaterial, DrawThreefold, DrawFiftyMoves; `kFiftyMovePlies` (100), the one threshold of the 50-move draw, which `Engine/Search.cpp` uses too
│   ├── StatusText.*          the words of the HUD: Turn(model, opponentThinking = false) ("White to move", "White wins", "Draw"; "Computer is thinking" while the game is on and the program searches) and Status(model) ("", "Check", "Checkmate", "Draw: threefold repetition", ...)
│   ├── BoardOrientation.h    where a square is drawn, pure and header only: `BoardCell` (column, rowFromTop), `BottomColor(programPlays)` (Black exactly when the program plays White, else White), `CellOf(square, bottom)` and its inverse `SquareAt(column, rowFromTop, bottom)` (nothing outside 0..7); Black at the bottom is the White grid turned by 180 degrees
│   ├── PromotionChoices.*    the four pieces a pawn can become, pure: PromotionPieces() (queen, rook, bishop, knight), PromotionId(type) ("queen", "rook", "bishop", "knight"), PromotionLabel(type) ("Queen", ...), FindPromotion(id) (exact, lower case); one table, which the promotion panel is held to
│   └── ChessGameModel.*      selected square, legal moves of it, status, move history, version, piece handles, the pending promotion; OnSquareClicked drives select / move (a click on a promotion target moves nothing: it leaves the promotion pending, HasPendingPromotion() / GetPromotionTarget(), and any other click drops it), ChoosePromotion(PieceType) plays the pending one (`IllegalMove` when none is pending or the piece is not one of the four), CancelPromotion() drops it and keeps the selection, TryMove(Move) is the one place a move is applied (a promotion by UCI or the program names its piece), SetPosition(Position) starts a game from a FEN, ClearSelection() drops a half-made move and a pending promotion for an owner that takes the turn; a pending promotion is not a change of the game, so the version does not move
├── Engine/                   the opponent, a program that picks a move (ChessGame owns its controller, ChessBoardComponent runs it; the opponent panel, opened by the Opponent button of the HUD, picks the mode, the person's color, the engine and the strength); depends on Game/ and on nothing in Components/
│   ├── ChessEngine.h         IChessEngine::FindMove(request, token): the interface; EngineError (a kind from EngineErrorKind and a sentence), SearchLimits (move time, depth)
│   ├── SearchRequest.*       SearchRequest: start FEN + moves + limits, from a model (FromModel) or from a position (FromPosition); Validate() the limits, FinalPosition() the position the moves lead to
│   ├── UciProtocol.*         the UCI text, pure: PositionCommand, GoCommand, SetOptionCommand, ParseBestMove, TrimLine
│   ├── UciProcess.*          a child process talked to in lines: Start(ProcessLaunch), WriteLine, ReadLine(timeout), Close(grace); the transport under a UCI engine, no UCI in it. POSIX only
│   ├── UciEngine.*           UciEngine: IChessEngine over UciProcess (handshake, ucinewgame, position / go / bestmove, timeouts, cancellation, a new process after an error); UciEngineSettings; FindStockfish(path): the explicit path, $STOCKFISH, stockfish on PATH
│   ├── EngineOpponent.*      EngineOpponent: plays one side of a ChessGameModel against any IChessEngine; Update(model) on the main thread starts the search on the Background pool and plays the answer with TryMove; IsThinking, Cancel, GetLastError / ClearError
│   ├── OpponentSettings.*    OpponentSettings: the path of Stockfish from `config/ChessSettings.xml` and the git-ignored `config/ChessSettings.local.xml` (FromConfig over two XmlNodes, Load() from ConfigManager; `ChessGame::Start()` calls it)
│   ├── OpponentEngines.*     the engine the person picks: `OpponentEngine` (`Stockfish`, `Own`), `kDefaultOpponentEngine` (Stockfish), OpponentEngineId (the wire name, "stockfish" / "own"), OpponentEngineName (the word of the HUD, "Stockfish" / "Own engine"), FindOpponentEngine(id), NextOpponentEngine (the cycle of `OpponentController::CycleEngine`; the HUD has no Engine button any more)
│   ├── BlunderEngine.*       `BlunderEngine`: the handicap, an `IChessEngine` over another one (`shared_ptr` + a percent clamped to 0..100). For a request it decides whether this move is a blunder and then answers a random legal move without asking the wrapped engine (no search, no process for a blunder); otherwise it delegates unchanged. **The decision and the move are a pure function of the position and the percent** (a splitmix64-style mix of the position's key, a salt and the number of moves of the request; no clock, no random device, no state), so they are the same on every run and platform, the manual-clock smoke stays reproducible, it is thread safe, and a position that blunders at 15 % also blunders at 40 %. The same position **reached by the same number of moves of the request** blunders the same way every time (a `FromPosition` request counts 0; the same position after a different number of moves may not). The contract of `IChessEngine` stays (`BadRequest`, `NoMove`, `Cancelled` on the blunder path as `OwnEngine` answers them); 0 % answers everything through the wrapped engine, errors included, 100 % never asks it. Made by `WithHandicap` (below) in the default factory of `OpponentController`; **a test double given by `SetEngineFactory` is never wrapped**
│   ├── OpponentLevels.*      the strengths of the opponent: easy, medium, hard, max (id, name, `Skill Level` and move time for Stockfish, `ownDepth` for the own engine, and `blunderPercent`, the share of moves a random legal move replaces, for both engines). The shipped numbers (2026-11-05, hand-tuned guesses that the owner confirms by playing, none of them measured as an Elo): easy Skill 0 / 50 ms / 40 % / depth 1, medium Skill 0 / 100 ms / 15 % / depth 2, hard Skill 5 / 300 ms / 0 % / depth 3, max Skill 20 / 2000 ms / 0 % / depth 5. **Why a handicap:** Stockfish at `Skill Level` 0 is about a 1300-1400 player and `UCI_Elo` at its minimum (1320) is the same strength, so neither goes lower (that is why `UCI_Elo`, `UCI_LimitStrength`, a node limit and `go depth` are not used), and a casual person is 600-1000; the own engine at depth 1 is about as good as Skill 0. A random legal move costs about five pawns, so `blunderPercent` is the one knob that reaches a beginner, and it is the same for both engines. The four ids and the wire are unchanged; a fifth "Beginner" segment is **not** added (the four segments of the `LevelGroup` are about 111 units each in the 640-unit plate and a fifth would break the 8 units of air that `ChessUiTest` demands: it needs a second row or a shorter word, the panel tests and the web twin, the owner's call). FindOpponentLevel(id), WithHandicap(engine, level) (the same pointer when `blunderPercent` is 0, else a `BlunderEngine` around it), LimitsOf(engine, level) -> SearchLimits (Stockfish: the move time; Own: the depth of the level, a fixed depth is deterministic, with `kOwnMoveTimeCapMs` = 10 s as the protection of the window, not a strength knob), EngineOptionsOf(level) -> UciEngineSettings::options (UCI only)
│   ├── OpponentText.*        the words of the HUD for the opponent, pure (`Game/` must not know the engine, so they are not in `StatusText`): SideLabel(computerPlays) (the text of the Opponent button: "Two players", "You: White", "You: Black"), ModeLabel(computer) ("Two players", "Computer") and ColorLabel(color) ("White", "Black", the color of the person), which with `OpponentEngineName` and `OpponentLevel::name` are the words of the segments of the opponent panel (one source, so a test can hold the scene to it), Error(EngineError) (a sentence of at most `kMaxErrorLength` = 28 characters per `EngineErrorKind`, a `switch` without `default`: "Engine not found", "Engine did not answer", "Illegal engine move", ...; none is wider on the HUD than the longest status of the game)
│   ├── Evaluation.*          the static evaluation of the engine of our own: `Evaluate(position)` in centipawns from the side to move (material P 100, N 320, B 330, R 500, Q 900 plus piece-square tables, a king table for the middlegame and one for the endgame by `IsEndgame`), pure and deterministic, the start position is 0; the scores of the search: `kMateScore`, `kDrawScore`, `MateIn(ply)`, `IsMateScore`
│   ├── Search.*              `Search(position, SearchSettings, SearchControl)`: iterative deepening negamax with alpha-beta, quiescence (captures, promotions, check evasions) and a one-ply check extension; best move of the last finished iteration first, then MVV-LVA, promotions, generator order (a stable sort); mate scores carry the distance; draws in the tree (stalemate, insufficient material, 50 moves, a repeated position with the game's history); stops on a `CancellationToken`, a deadline or a node limit, polled every `kSearchPollInterval` nodes, and hands the position back equal. **Deterministic for a fixed depth** (no table, no randomness, no clock): the same position and depth give the same move, score and node count on every platform; a search cut by a deadline or a token is not deterministic.
│   ├── OwnEngine.*           `OwnEngine`: `IChessEngine` over `Search`, in the process of the game and on every target, with the contract of `UciEngine` minus the process (`BadRequest`, `NoMove`, `Cancelled`, a deadline is not an error; the move is checked against the generator before it leaves). `SearchLimits::depth` is the last iteration (deterministic), `moveTimeMs` a deadline (not deterministic), both: the earlier. No state between calls, so threads may share one object. **Picked by the person** in the opponent panel, the Engine row (or `opponent.engine` of the control API); the default engine is still Stockfish and nothing falls back to it: see "The opponent in the game"
│   ├── OpponentChoice.*      OpponentChoice: the choice as the opponent panel makes it, over an `OpponentController&` (no scene): `OpponentMode` (`Two`, `Computer`; ids "two", "computer"), the color of the PERSON (ids "white", "black"; the control API's `plays` is the PROGRAM's color, the opposite one), the engine and the level by their ids of the control API; SetMode / SetColor / SetEngine / SetLevel(id) -> `std::expected<void, ChoiceError>` (`BadId`: the id is in no table, nothing changed and no engine asked; `Engine`: the controller refused the pick, its `EngineError` inside), Mode / Color / ModeId / ColorId / EngineId / LevelId, Reset (White); `PieceColorId` and `ParseColor` are shared with `ChessGameControl`; see "The choice of the person"
│   └── OpponentController.*  OpponentController: the seat of the opponent for one ChessGameModel; SetSide / SetLevel / SetEngine / Choose (side and level, or side, level and engine, in one pick) remake the EngineOpponent behind the choice, and so do CycleSide / CycleEngine / CycleLevel (the HUD no longer calls them: it has the panel; kept and tested), Update() per frame, PlaysFor(color), GetEngine, IsThinking, GetError, GetRevision, Shutdown; EngineFactory makes the engine for (engine, level) (default: a UciEngine over the Stockfish of OpponentSettings for `Stockfish`, an OwnEngine for `Own`)
└── Components/               scene glue (IComponent)
    ├── ChessBoardComponent   draws the board, the check and the highlights, turns mouse clicks into OnSquareClicked (and restarts after the game ends; ignores a click while the program plays the side to move), runs the opponent's Update() on every SceneUpdateEvent and logs the result of a game once, publishes `turn`, `status`, `side`, `opponentMode`, `opponentColor`, `opponentEngine`, `opponentLevel` and `opponentError` to UIVars when the model, the opponent's revision, its search or the person's color changed, answers the actions ChessReset, ChessOpenOpponent / ChessCloseOpponent (show and hide the canvas of the node `OpponentPanel`) and ChessSetMode / ChessSetColor / ChessSetEngine / ChessSetLevel (the `value` of the event is an id, picked through `OpponentChoice`; an id in no table is ignored with one warning), ChessPromote (the `value` is the id of a piece of `PromotionChoices`; `ChoosePromotion` plays it, an id in no table is ignored with one warning) and ChessCancelPromotion (`CancelPromotion`); shows the canvas of the node `PromotionPanel` while the model has a pending promotion and hides it otherwise, after a click on the board and on every SceneUpdateEvent (so a reset, a `set_state` or a move of someone else closes it), only when that differs from what it set last
    ├── ChessPieceComponent   logical handle of one piece; drives its TransformComponent from the model and switches its sprite after a promotion
    └── ChessResultComponent  on its own node (layer 1, over the pieces): dims the board and draws the result bar once the game is over
```

- State lives in `ChessGameModel`. Components never keep game state: they reach the model through `ChessGame::GetActiveModel()` (null when Chess is not the active game, and then they do nothing).
- A piece node in `ChessScene` is a `ChessPieceComponent` + `SpriteRendererComponent` (artwork from `TextureLibrary`, `config/TextureLibrary.xml`) + `TransformComponent`. `OnAttached` registers the piece with the model at its starting square (a piece that registers late, in the first frame, after a client has already moved, is dealt to the position as it stands: `RegisterPiece` re-deals when the game has left the opening); `Reset()` sends every handle back. The model moves a handle with its piece through captures, castling (the rook too), en passant (the passed pawn's handle is the one removed) and promotion (the handle reports the new type and the component switches the sprite to `<Color><Type>`, e.g. `WhiteQueen`).
- **The node is the whole square, the sprite is fitted inside it.** `ChessPieceComponent` writes the node's position and `_width = _height = squareSize` every update, and the sprite of each of the 32 piece nodes is `fit="Contain"` (`SpriteFit`, `engine/src/Modules/BECore/Scene/CLAUDE.md`): the piece is drawn inside the square with the aspect of its atlas cell (116.67 x 190.5, tall), as high as the square and centered. The board fills the selection, legal-move and check highlights over the same full square, so they sit around the piece and `ChessBoardComponent` does not need to know the size of the piece. A new piece node needs the attribute (`Stretch`, the default, squashes the tall cell into the square; a test counts the 32).
- The piece atlas (`assets/chess_test.png`) must keep a real alpha channel: a sprite with an opaque background paints a box over its square and hides the square color and the highlights. A replacement atlas needs a transparent background. Its cells may have any aspect: the fit keeps whatever `srcRect` the library gives, so the cell size in `TextureLibrary.xml` and the art can change without touching the scene.
- Board geometry (`_originX`, `_originY`, `_squareSize`) is reflected so it can be tuned in the scene editor. It is in scene units, not window pixels: `ChessScene` declares `viewWidth="840" viewHeight="800"` (the board 640x640 at (100, 80): 100 units on the sides and 80 above and below, where the HUD sits), so the engine scales the whole scene to the window and centers it. Change the view size together with the board geometry, and map mouse positions with `Scene::ScreenToSceneX/Y` (`OnMouseDown` does).
- **The HUD** is a `SceneView` canvas on the `Hud` node (layer 10, over the end-of-game dim), in the two margins of the view, 12 units from the edge of the safe area, every control 56 units high and anchored to a corner. Top row: `Turn` (label `{turn}`, left, 320 wide, font 28), `Opponent` (button `{side}`, action `ChessOpenOpponent`, `anchor="TopRight" x="-134"`, 190 wide, font 24, blue) and `Reset` (button, `ChessReset`, 110 wide, font 26, green). Bottom row: `Status` (label `{status}`, bottom right, `48%` wide, right aligned, font 28) alone; the bottom left is empty. `Opponent` is **always shown** and says the state (the three words of `{side}`: "Two players", "You: White", "You: Black"); one press opens the opponent panel (next bullet). The HUD scales and moves with the board. That it stays outside the board is checked in five window shapes without a safe-area inset (`TheHudStaysOutsideTheBoardWithoutInsets`); with a notch or a home-bar inset in a landscape or square window a row can reach onto the edge of the board (the old HUD did too). **Why one row per margin:** the margin is 80 units, a 56-unit button is a 32 px touch target at the smallest fit scale of the size matrix (0.57, because the view is 840 units wide); a second row would need a taller view and a smaller board. **The width budget** is set by the narrowest case of the matrix, a 480 px wide window with side insets (9 % each side): scale 0.571, a safe area of about 689 units. Top row: `Turn` ends at 332, `Opponent` spans about 365 to 555 and `Reset` 567 to 677 (gaps of 33 and 12); "Computer is thinking" needs 249 of the 320, and `ChessUiTest` lays each of the three words of `{side}` into the 190 units of `Opponent` with 8 units of air. Bottom row: `Status` is about 330.7 wide from about 346; the widest sentence is "Engine not supported here" at 318.9 of 330.6 ("Draw: insufficient material" is close behind; the old "Engine made an illegal move" needed 347 and was reworded to "Illegal engine move"). Those numbers were measured when the bottom row also held two buttons: the geometry and the words of `Turn`, `Reset` and `Status` have not changed since, and `Opponent` has the slot the old `Level` button had; `Status` keeps its `48%` although the bottom row is free now, and nobody has re-measured. A wider window only adds room (the anchors are corners and `Status` is a percent), so the narrowest case decides. **Thinking and errors:** "Computer is thinking" is the `Turn` label and "Black to move" comes back when the answer is played; the engine's error is the `Status` label (and the error line of the panel) and wins over "Check" while it lasts. While an error stands `Turn` still says whose move it is ("Black to move") and only `Status` explains; the sentence says what went wrong and never what to do next (the README section "Playing against the computer" does: Reset, "Two players", the path). The window never freezes without a word: a search says so, a failure says so, and Reset, a pick in the panel or a move of someone else lets the person out. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` runs the HUD through the size matrix with every word the game can show, and the words come from the tables through `tests/HudWords.h` (`StatusText` over a scratch game, `OpponentText`, every `EngineErrorKind`), never a copy: the font is proportional, so the longest word by characters is not always the widest and each word gets a round. The validator's `TextDoesNotFit` does not see a label that cuts its text with an ellipsis (a label with `Ellipsis` "can shrink"), so the test asks `CheckContent().fits` of every shown control itself (a button must also keep 8 units of air between its text and the edge of its fill) and demands 8 units between any two controls. `ChessHudWebTest` holds the same four elements written in HTML + CSS to the same rects.
- **The opponent panel** is the node `OpponentPanel` (layer 20, over the HUD and the result bar): a `SceneView` canvas started `visible="false"` and `modal="true"`, written after the engine's template for a choice panel (`engine/tests/BECore/UISelectorScreenTest.cpp`, `kSelectorXml`; `engine/src/Modules/BECore/UI/CLAUDE.md`, "Choice panels"). A full-screen `Dim` (`blocksInput`) and a `Plate` (`anchor="Center" width="94%" maxWidth="640"`, 460 units high: 16 + 44 title + 4 x 56 rows + 32 error line + 56 OK + 6 x 12 spacing + 16; the view is a fixed 840 x 800, so there is no portrait variant) with the `Title`, four rows, the `OpponentError` label (height 32, font 22, red text `{opponentError}`, centered) and `Ok`. A row is a caption (150 wide) beside a `UISegmented` group of 56-unit `UIButton` segments (a segment needs an explicit `height`: a flex segment without one gets a rect of height 0). The groups, with the ids of the control API as the `value` of their segments: `ModeGroup` ("Opponent": `Two players` = `two`, `Computer` = `computer`; bind `opponentMode`, action `ChessSetMode`), `ColorGroup` ("Play as": `White` = `white`, `Black` = `black`; `opponentColor`, `ChessSetColor`), `EngineGroup` ("Engine": `Stockfish` = `stockfish`, `Own engine` = `own`; `opponentEngine`, `ChessSetEngine`) and `LevelGroup` ("Level": `Easy`, `Medium`, `Hard`, `Max` = `easy` .. `max`; `opponentLevel`, `ChessSetLevel`). The engine's `UISegmented` marks, on every draw, the segment whose `value` equals the bound variable as `selected`, and a press on a segment only emits its action with its `value`: the group owns no choice, the board answers the action and publishes the variable, so the panel always shows what the game really has. The shipped XML sets no `selectedColor`: the look of the selected segment is the engine's derived default, a first guess nobody has seen in a window yet. **The color is the PERSON's color**; the control API's `opponent.plays` is the PROGRAM's, the opposite one (the program plays Black = the person has White), and the panel never shows `plays`. **A pick applies at once and the panel stays open** (each press is one pick on the `OpponentController`: a new engine, the running search dropped, by the controller's own rules); only OK (`ChessCloseOpponent`) closes it. The `Opponent` button emits `ChessOpenOpponent` and `ChessBoardComponent` shows the canvas with `UICanvasComponent::SetVisibleByName`. While it is shown the dim, the plate and the segments take every press (`blocksInput` and `modal`), so the board and the Reset button under it get none. A panel left open when the game ends, on Reset or on a `set_state` of the control API stays open and is closed only by OK (there is no Escape and no click outside to close; OK is always reachable). **No group is greyed:** every group of the panel takes clicks in every mode. `ColorGroup` and `LevelGroup` were `disabled` (`UISegmented::disabled`) while two people played, because they mean something only when the program plays, but the panel ate the click and nothing happened, which the owner read as a dead button ("Max sometimes sticks"); a pick made while two people play is now remembered (the controller keeps the level, `OpponentChoice` the person's color), shown as selected, and applies when Computer is picked. The engine's `UISegmented::disabled` stays a feature of the widget, this game does not use it. **A pick that fails is refused:** an engine that cannot be had cannot be saved (no fallback, nothing switches the engine silently, and the pick does not switch the program off either): "Computer" with no Stockfish found changes nothing, so `opponentMode` stays `two` (the screen shows the truth); a Stockfish pick while Own engine plays as Computer keeps Computer, Own engine, the level and the running search (an owner's decision of 2026-11-05: choosing Stockfish used to switch the mode to Two players). The controller keeps the error, and "Engine not found" stands in the `OpponentError` line and in `Status` (until a move of someone else, a reset, a `set_state` or the next pick; the program's own move does not take it away, or it would be gone before anyone read it); the way on is `Own engine` in `EngineGroup` (it clears the error) and `Computer` again. **The names:** the groups are `ModeGroup`, `ColorGroup`, `EngineGroup` and `LevelGroup` and not `Mode`, `Engine` and `Level`, because elements are found by name in every canvas of the scene (`UICanvasComponent::SetElementVisibleByName`, `SetVisibleByName`, the lookups of the tests); a test checks that no name is in both canvases and that the old `Side`, `Engine` and `Level` elements and any `ChessCycle*` action are gone from `SceneConfig.xml`. **Why a panel and not buttons:** the margins are full (the width budget above, and a second row does not fit), and the owner found one cycle button for the mode and the color bad (they are two choices, each should show its current value and be reachable in one press). The engine has the widgets for it now (`UISegmented`, `UIToggle`, `UISlider`, `UIProgressBar`, `UIStrings`, and `ui_tree` / `ui_click` in the control API); when the cycle buttons were made it had none, which is why they were cycle buttons. **How the layout is held:** `ChessUiTest` copies the checks of the template on the shipped scene (the validator on the size matrix, then `CheckContent().fits` with 8 units of air of every control on each case of the matrix and in the narrowest window alone, with every sentence of `opponentError`; 10 segments, each at least 56 units high and a 32 px touch target in the narrowest window; the plate inside the view and centered in five window shapes) and builds broken copies from the text of the shipped XML so the checks can fail. `ChessHudWebTest` holds the plate written in HTML + CSS (`data-widget="segmented"`, `data-bind`, `data-value`, `data-action`) to the same rects and attributes.
- **The promotion picker.** A click on a promotion target does not move the pawn and never plays the queen on its own: `ChessGameModel::OnSquareClicked` leaves the promotion **pending in the model** (state of the game, not of a component; `HasPendingPromotion()`, `GetPromotionTarget()`), and the person picks the piece in the node `PromotionPanel`. The pieces, their ids and their words are one table, `Game/PromotionChoices.*` (`queen`, `rook`, `bishop`, `knight`; "Queen", "Rook", "Bishop", "Knight"; queen first), which the scene is held to. The panel is a `SceneView` canvas on layer 20 started `visible="false"` and `modal="true"`, written after the opponent panel: a full-screen `PromotionDim` (`blocksInput`) and a `PromotionPlate` (`anchor="Center" width="94%" maxWidth="640"`, 224 units high: 16 + 44 title + 56 row of pieces + 56 Cancel + 3 x 12 spacing + 16) with the `PromotionTitle` ("Promote to"), the row `PromotionPieces` of four 56-unit buttons `PromoteQueen` / `PromoteRook` / `PromoteBishop` / `PromoteKnight` (`action="ChessPromote"`, the `value` is the id) and `PromotionCancel` (`ChessCancelPromotion`). 94 % of the safe area, even of the narrowest one (about 689 units), is more than 640, so the plate is 640 wide in every window of the size matrix and a piece button 146 units, which keeps 8 units of air around "Knight" at font 26. The names all start with `Promotion` or `Promote` because elements are found by name in every canvas of the scene (a test holds that no name is in two of the three canvases). **Shown by the model:** `ChessBoardComponent` shows the canvas while `HasPendingPromotion()` is true and hides it otherwise, after a click on the board and on every `SceneUpdateEvent` (only when that differs from what it set last), so a Reset, a `set_state`, a move of the program or of the control API, or the program taking the turn closes a panel that went stale; `ClearSelection()` (so `TryMove`, `Reset`, `SetPosition`) drops the pending choice, and any click on a square of the board cancels it in the model. A press on a piece button emits `ChessPromote` and the board plays `ChoosePromotion` for the id (an id in no table, empty or of the wrong case is ignored with one `LOG_WARNING` and the panel stays); Cancel emits `ChessCancelPromotion` and the pawn stays selected, so the same target asks again. While the panel is shown the dim, the plate and the buttons take every press (`modal`, `blocksInput`), so the board, the Reset button and the Opponent button under it get none. The control API, UCI and the program's moves never see the panel: `TryMove` and `perform_action` name the piece themselves (`e7e8q`), and the version does not move when a promotion becomes pending or is cancelled. **Not done:** no piece pictures in the buttons (plain text; a product choice), no Escape and no click outside to cancel (Cancel only, as OK of the opponent panel), no "always queen" option, and **no HTML + CSS twin in `ChessHudWebTest`** (that file holds the HUD and the opponent panel; the layout of this panel is held on the shipped scene by `ChessUiTest` alone, which was left out on purpose to keep the slice bounded). The look in a real window has been seen only in tests.

- **The opponent** (`Engine/`). `IChessEngine::FindMove` is synchronous and blocking: it returns when the engine has answered, was cancelled (`CancellationToken`) or failed, so a caller that must not wait runs it on a worker. It takes a `SearchRequest` (the start FEN of the game and the moves played, plus the limits), not a `Position`: UCI engines see repetitions only through `position fen <startFen> moves ...`, which a bare `Position` cannot give (`SearchRequest::FromPosition` drops the history, `FromModel` keeps it). Errors are values, `std::expected<Move, EngineError>`; a move that comes back is legal in the position the request leads to. `UciProtocol` writes the same `position` command as `position_command` of `tools/stockfish_bridge.py`. `UciProcess` is the child process under it (POSIX; Windows answers `Unsupported`): the child gets pipes for stdin and stdout, `/dev/null` for stderr and no other descriptor (the control socket of the game stays out of it; this holds on macOS and with glibc 2.34 or later, on older glibc and musl a descriptor of the game without close-on-exec is inherited, and Linux is not built or tested here), and a write to a dead child is `ProcessDied`, not `SIGPIPE` (on macOS the pipe carries `F_SETNOSIGPIPE`; elsewhere `Start()` sets `SIGPIPE` to ignored for the whole process, which the header says).
- **The UCI engine** (`UciEngine`) is `IChessEngine` over `UciProcess`, the port of `UciEngine` of the bridge. **The path comes from the caller** (`UciEngineSettings::launch`); `FindStockfish(path)` only looks for one, like `find_stockfish` of the bridge: the explicit path (it must be a file; when it is not there is no fallback), then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`. Nothing here knows where Stockfish is installed and nothing of it is shipped. Stockfish is GPL-3.0: it is neither linked nor copied, none of its code or files enter this repository or `engine/`, it is a separate program the user installs and the game starts through a path. The constructor starts nothing; the process starts on the first `FindMove` and again on the first one after an error closed it. One search at a time (a mutex); the waits go in slices of 20 ms, so a cancelled `CancellationToken` is seen soon. Failures are `EngineError` kinds: `BadRequest` (limits or request wrong; no process is started), `NoMove` (the game is over, which the engine is not asked, or it answered `bestmove (none)` / `0000`), `IllegalMove` (a move the position does not allow, its text in `detail`), `LaunchFailed`, `Timeout` (no `uciok` / `readyok` within `startupTimeoutMs`, no `bestmove` within `movetime + answerSlackMs`, or `depthOnlyTimeoutMs` for a depth without time), `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError`, `Cancelled` (`stop` is sent and the answer to it is read and dropped), `Unsupported` (Windows). **What closes the process** (`quit`, `quitGraceMs`, then `SIGTERM` and `SIGKILL`; always reaped): `Timeout`, `ProcessDied`, `ProtocolError`, a cancelled search whose engine does not answer `stop` within `stopGraceMs`, a cancel during the setup (`uciok` / `readyok`, `ucinewgame` + `isready`: no search runs yet, so no `stop` is sent, the process is closed and `Cancelled` comes back once the process has gone: at once for an engine that obeys `quit`, after the `quitGraceMs` and the signals for one that does not), and the destructor (on the thread that destroys the engine, up to the `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s for the signals). `NoMove`, `IllegalMove` and a cancelled search the engine did answer keep it. `ucinewgame` goes with the first search of a process, another start FEN, or moves that do not extend the previous request's.
- **The opponent in a game** (`EngineOpponent(engine, side, limits)`, any `IChessEngine`: the UCI one, a test double, `OwnEngine` is one, which the person can pick in the window). **Threads:** the main thread calls `Update(model)` once per frame and it never waits for the engine. When it is the opponent's turn, the game is not over, no search is running and no error is waiting, `Update` copies the game (`SearchRequest::FromModel`) and hands `FindMove` to `TaskManager::Run` (the Background pool; a call that blocks for seconds belongs there); a later `Update` finds the answer and plays it with `TryMove`. **The model is read and written only in `Update`, on the main thread**; the worker has a copy of the request and a state of its own (the engine, a `CancellationToken`, the answer behind a mutex, a done flag) held by `eastl::shared_ptr` and passed by value to the coroutine, so nothing dangles whichever side ends first. One search at a time. **A stale answer is dropped:** the model's `GetVersion()` at the time of the request is remembered, and when it has moved (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the running search is cancelled and its answer thrown away, also when the engine ignores the token. A failed search (any `EngineError`; a `Cancelled` of a search the opponent itself cancelled is dropped, not an error, while an engine that returns `Cancelled` without the opponent having cancelled is recorded as one, and no retry loop follows) or a move the model refuses is logged once (`LOG_ERROR`; with `OpponentController` the only places in `Engine/` that log), kept in `GetLastError()`, and no search starts until `ClearError()`: a missing engine is one error, not a try per frame. The destructor and `Cancel()` cancel the token and return at once, they never `Wait()`; a search that ignores its token keeps the engine alive until it returns (the engine, and its child process, are then closed on the pool thread). `IsThinking()` is true from the `Update` that starts a search to the one that takes its answer, and false once it is cancelled. One opponent serves one model, and the `TaskManager` must be running. **Shutdown:** destroy the opponent (or `Cancel()` it) before `TaskManager::Shutdown()` and call no `Update()` after it. `Shutdown()` joins the pool, so a running search whose token nobody cancelled holds the join until the engine's own deadline (`movetime` + `answerSlackMs`, 15 s or more by default); a search still queued is run before the join too (the workers drain their queues), with the same wait. An `Update()` after the shutdown starts a search that the scheduler drops (`engine/src/Modules/TaskSystem/CLAUDE.md`): its `done` never rises and `IsThinking()` stays true. The destructor never touches `TaskManager`, so destroying the opponent after the shutdown is safe.
- **The settings file.** Where a person's Stockfish is comes from two config files with one key, the attribute `stockfishPath` of the root element. `config/ChessSettings.xml` is committed and its value is empty (a test reads it from the source tree and fails on a path, so a person's path cannot be committed by mistake); `config/ChessSettings.local.xml` is git-ignored and is where a person puts their own. They are two files with two stems because `ConfigManager` keys a file by its stem and two files with the same stem inside one layer collide (the one scanned later wins), so a local override cannot be `config/local/ChessSettings.xml`: `ChessSettings.local` is a name of its own. The local value wins when it is not empty; a missing file, attribute or value is "not set" and never an error. The lookup order is therefore the config line, then `$STOCKFISH`, then `stockfish` on `PATH`: `OpponentSettings::stockfishPath` is the explicit path of `FindStockfish`, an empty one makes it go on to the other two, and a non-empty one that names no file is not skipped (`FindStockfish` has no fallback after a typo). Use an absolute path; a relative one is taken from the working directory of the game. `OpponentSettings::FromConfig(shared, local)` is pure over two `XmlNode` (the name of the root element is not checked, the stem is the key; the values are trimmed), `Load()` reads the two configs from `ConfigManager` and needs the engine services. The game's `config/` is mounted in the executable and not in the gtest environment, so the tests feed `FromConfig` strings. The strength table is `OpponentLevels.*`; only `Skill Level` and the move time are used for Stockfish (`UCI_Elo` has the same floor as Skill 0 and buys nothing; see the layout line), the handicap of `blunderPercent` goes under both engines, and the numbers are hand-tuned guesses that the owner confirms by playing, not measured strengths (the yardsticks of `StockfishGameTest` are small samples). `OpponentController` takes the settings from its caller (`SetSettings`) and uses the table; `ChessGame::Start()` calls `OpponentSettings::Load()` and hands the result to its controller (the tests that start the game through `GameManager` get an empty path: nothing is mounted there, and their engines come from `SetEngineFactory`).
- **The seat of the opponent** (`OpponentController(model, settings)`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it). It holds the choice, which side **the program** plays (`GetPlays()`; none = two people on one screen) which strength (`GetLevel()`, an index of `GetOpponentLevels()`; `kDefaultOpponentLevel`, also while nobody plays) and which engine (`GetEngine()`, an `OpponentEngine`: `Stockfish` or `Own`; `kDefaultOpponentEngine` = `Stockfish`, also while nobody plays), and the `EngineOpponent` behind it. A game starts with nobody: nothing is looked up or started until a side is picked. `SetSide(side or nothing)`, `SetLevel(index)`, `SetEngine(engine)`, `CycleSide()` (nobody -> the program plays Black, so the person has White -> the program plays White -> nobody), `CycleEngine()` (Stockfish -> Own -> Stockfish) and `CycleLevel()` (wraps) destroy the old `EngineOpponent` first (it cancels its search and returns at once; its late answer is never played, also from an engine that ignores its token), ask the engine factory for an engine for `(engine, level)` and make a new `EngineOpponent` with `LimitsOf(engine, level)`: the side is fixed by the `EngineOpponent` constructor and `Skill Level` is sent at the handshake, so a change needs a new engine. While nobody plays, `SetEngine` / `CycleEngine` only change the choice (no factory call, nothing looked up or started) and, like any pick, clear a standing pick error. A pick that changes nothing (the same side, level and engine, no error standing) does nothing, so a search in flight is not thrown away and a running Stockfish is not restarted (this holds for the control API's `set_state` too; with a pick error standing the same side goes through the factory again, which is the retry, and nobody clears it); `Choose(side, level)` (the engine kept) and `Choose(side, level, engine)` are the whole choice in one pick (one engine made, not two or three); when the program takes the side to move, `ChessGameModel::ClearSelection()` drops the person's half-made move. **The factory** (`SetEngineFactory`, an `EngineFactory`: engine and level in, engine or `EngineError` out) is how every test avoids a real Stockfish; the default (an empty factory) makes, for `Stockfish`, a `UciEngine` over `FindStockfish(stockfishPath)` with `options = EngineOptionsOf(level)` and `quitGraceMs = OpponentController::kQuitGraceMs` (500), and for `Own` an `OwnEngine` (in the process, no process, no file looked for, no settings read, it cannot fail), **each wrapped by `WithHandicap(engine, level)`** (a `BlunderEngine` for `easy` and `medium`, the engine itself for `hard` and `max`; a factory given by a test is never wrapped, so the doubles of `TestEngines.h` stay exact). The old engine is dropped on the main thread when no search is running, and `~UciEngine` waits there for a stuck child up to `quitGraceMs` plus 0.5 s, so a level change blocks the main thread for 1 s at most (the default of `UciEngineSettings` is 5 s; an `OwnEngine` is dropped at once). `SetSettings` and a new factory are read only at the next real pick. **A pick of Stockfish succeeds whenever `FindStockfish` finds a file:** the program is not started until its first search, so a file that is not an engine or not executable shows later, as `LaunchFailed` of that first search (`GetError()`, `opponent.error` on the wire); the game then waits, the program's side stays locked for the person until the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move through the control API) or another pick is made. **A pick that finds no engine is refused** (the factory fails; for the default one `LaunchFailed`, whose detail names the path that was tried and says that `~` and environment variables are not expanded, or the three places when the path is empty; the own engine cannot fail): the engine is asked for FIRST, and when there is none **nothing changes**: the side, the level, the engine and the running `EngineOpponent` with its search stay as they were (an engine that cannot be had cannot be saved; `Choose(side, level, engine)` is all or nothing), the error is kept in `GetError()` (the refused pick's error wins over the opponent's own), the revision grows once and one `LOG_WARNING` names the engine. The old opponent goes on playing while the refused pick's error stands (that error does not stop it); a game that started with nobody stays with two people, so the person is not locked out. A pick that needs no engine (nobody, or `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` while nobody plays) cannot fail. Picking again what already plays, with only a refused pick's error standing, just clears the error (nothing is remade). **No fallback of any kind:** nothing picks the other engine for the person (the owner's decision: the engine is chosen in the window, never silently), so a missing Stockfish stays "Engine not found" until the person picks Own engine in the panel (which clears the error) and then Computer. **The error rule:** an error belongs to the `GetVersion()` of the model it came from (a failed pick, or the opponent's `GetLastError()` noted by the `Update()` that took the answer). When the version moves (a reset, a `set_state`, a move of someone else) the next `Update()` clears the error, the opponent's too (`ClearError()`), and a new pick clears it as well: the person has acted, so they have seen it. Until then no search starts and nothing retries per frame; a broken engine is left by picking nobody (a search that failed; a refused pick leaves the old one playing). `PlaysFor(color)` stays true while an error stands (the side is still the program's). `GetRevision()` grows when the side, the level, the engine or the error changes, never with a search that starts or ends (`IsThinking()`) or a move (the version of the model), so a display watches the three. `Update()` is `EngineOpponent::Update(model)` once per frame, main thread. `Shutdown()` (the destructor too) destroys the opponent: nobody afterwards and `Update()` does nothing. All of it is for the main thread only. **The HUD does not call `CycleSide` / `CycleEngine` / `CycleLevel`** since the panel replaced the cycle buttons: it picks by id through `OpponentChoice` (`SetSide`, `SetEngine`, `SetLevel`). The cycle methods stay on the controller with their `OpponentControllerTest` cases.
- **The choice of the person** (`OpponentChoice(controller)`, in `Engine/`, no scene or component in it). The controller knows one thing about the sides, the color the PROGRAM plays (`GetPlays()`, none = two people), and so does the control API; the panel speaks of the PERSON: a mode (Two players / Computer) and the color the person plays (the program plays the other one). This class turns between the two views and **remembers the person's color while nobody plays**, when the controller holds no side. `SetMode("computer")` = `SetSide(opposite(Color()))`, `SetMode("two")` = `SetSide(nullopt)` (the color stays), `SetColor(c)` remembers `c` and, when the program plays, `SetSide(opposite(c))` (one new engine), `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` go to the controller as they are; the same pick again is the controller's no-op. The ids are exact (lower case): an empty one, `"White"`, a prefix or one with a trailing space is a `ChoiceError` of the kind `BadId`, nothing changes, no engine is asked and the revision stays; a refused pick is a `ChoiceError` of the kind `Engine` (the controller keeps the error and `Mode()` says Two, the remembered color is the one that was picked). `ChessGame` owns it (after the controller) and `Start()` calls `Reset()`, so a new game starts with White remembered. **`Color()` refreshes the remembered color from the controller:** while the program plays, the person's color is the opposite of `plays`, whoever set it, and that is remembered, so a `set_state` of the control API (`plays: "white"`) is followed by the panel (the person has Black) and `plays: null` afterwards keeps that color. `Color()` is a const getter that writes a mutable member; main thread only. `ChessBoardComponent` reads it on every `SceneUpdateEvent` and on every publication, and `SetMode` reads it before the side goes. **A known gap:** the refresh happens only when `Color()` is read, which is once per frame, so two `set_state` calls with no frame between them (`plays: "white"`, then `plays: null`) leave White remembered; it shows in `opponentColor` and in the side a later Computer pick gives, and on no path of a click.
- **The opponent in the game** (`ChessGame`, `ChessBoardComponent`). `ChessGame` owns the `OpponentController` after the model (`GetOpponent()`) and the `OpponentChoice` right after it (`GetChoice()`), both before the control adapter; `ChessGame::GetActive()`, `GetActiveModel()`, `GetActiveOpponent()` and `GetActiveChoice()` are how components and tests reach them (null when Chess is not the active game). **It is created when a side is picked:** `Start()` resets the model, loads the settings and sets nobody, the default level and the default engine (Stockfish), so a game with no engine configured plays as two people on one screen and nothing is looked up or started; an engine is made by the first `SetSide()` on `GetOpponent()` (what `SetMode("computer")` of the choice does); the board component calls `SetMode` / `SetColor` / `SetEngine` / `SetLevel` of the choice for the actions `ChessSetMode` / `ChessSetColor` / `ChessSetEngine` / `ChessSetLevel` with the `value` of the event (an id) and shows and hides the panel for `ChessOpenOpponent` / `ChessCloseOpponent`; the segments of the panel and the `Opponent` and OK buttons emit them. **The frame:** `IGame` has no per-frame hook, so `ChessBoardComponent` subscribes to `SceneEvents::SceneUpdateEvent` (`SubscribeInScene`) and calls `Update()` there, on the main thread; never in `OnDraw()`, which must not change the model. The program's move is played in that update. **The click guard:** `ChessBoardComponent::OnMouseDown` returns, after the "game over: a click starts the next game" branch, when `PlaysFor(model->GetTurn())`: `OnSquareClicked` does not know sides, and a person who selected the program's piece would only fight the program's own search. The guard holds while a search error stands too (`PlaysFor` stays true), so the person's ways out matter and are tested: the Reset button (the version moves, the error rule clears it), a pick in the panel (a color, engine or level: a new opponent), and picking Two players. The control API is not blocked for the program's side. **A new game needs nothing special:** the Reset button, a click after the end, the control `reset` and `set_state` move `GetVersion()`, which cancels the running search and drops its answer, and the program, when it plays the side to move, opens the new game. **Shutdown:** `ChessGame::Stop()` calls the controller's `Shutdown()`. `Application::Cleanup()` runs `GameManager::Shutdown()` (which calls `Stop()` and then destroys the games) before `CoreManager::OnApplicationDeinit()` shuts the task system down, so no search holds the join of the pool; `GameManager` calls `Stop()` when it switches away from a game as well. A test shuts the game down with a gated search that ignores its token, and it returns at once. The result of a game is logged once by whoever sees it first (`LogResultOnce`: the click that ended it, or the update after the move of the program or of the control API). The words and ids of the HUD and the panel (`side`, `opponentMode`, `opponentColor`, `opponentEngine`, `opponentLevel`, `opponentError`, "Computer is thinking", the error sentence) are published and the panel has the groups that pick the mode, the color, the engine and the level. **The control API** reports and sets the opponent (below). **The engine of our own** (`OwnEngine`, above) is the second engine, picked by the person in the Engine row of the panel (or `opponent.engine` of the control API): its levels are a depth each (`OpponentLevel::ownDepth`), it runs in the process on every target (Windows and the web included) and needs no install. **The default is Stockfish and nothing falls back:** a game with no Stockfish reports "Engine not found" until the person picks Own engine in the panel; a product choice for the owner is making `Own` the default (a one-line change of `kDefaultOpponentEngine`, which would still not be a fallback). Deferred, with the reason: the board does not flip when the person plays Black (a slice of its own), and takeback and resign wait for the Menu button of `todo/chess-opponent-button-name.md` (the margins of the HUD are full); the promotion picker exists (see its bullet) but has plain text buttons, no Escape or click outside to cancel and no web twin; the choice of side, engine and level is not remembered between runs (nothing asked for it); closing the panel with Escape or a click outside it (OK only); the live look of the panel in a real window (the selected segment is the engine's derived first guess, and the dim and the error line have been seen only in tests; a change of the widget would be an engine entry); Windows for Stockfish only (`UciProcess` is POSIX only); a transposition table or opening book for the own engine; the strength of the ladder in the Elo a person feels (only the owner can say, by playing; the yardstick tables are small samples against one reference); a fifth level at the bottom ("Beginner": it needs the width budget of the `LevelGroup`, a second row or a shorter word, the panel tests on the size matrix and the web twin); a weak move smarter than a pure-random one (a blunder that does not lose a piece at once); and the blunder is a pure function of the position, so **the same position (after the same number of moves of the request) blunders the same way every time**: a person who replays the line gets the same mistake, which is wanted for the manual clock and the tests and may or may not be wanted in the window.

## Control API

`ChessGame::AsControllable()` hands out `ChessGameControl` (`engine/src/Modules/Games/CLAUDE.md` for the methods and the rules of an adapter). The model and the opponent controller belong to the game, so the adapter holds references and has no `no_active_model`. The wire format is in the header comment of `ChessGameControl.h`; in short:

| Method | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| `get_state` | `{fen, board, turn, status, winner, inCheck, legalMoveCount, startFen, moves, opponent}`: `board` is 8 strings, rank 8 first, white upper case, `.` empty; `status` is `ongoing`, `checkmate`, `stalemate`, `draw_insufficient_material`, `draw_threefold` or `draw_fifty_moves`; `startFen` plus `moves` (UCI) rebuild the game with its repetitions; `opponent` is `{plays, level, engine, thinking, error}`: `plays` the color the **program** plays (`"white"`, `"black"`, or `null` for two people, the start of every game), `level` the id of the strength (`easy`, `medium`, `hard`, `max`; the one that would be used, also while `plays` is null), `engine` the id of the program (`stockfish`, the start of every game, or `own`; also the one that would be used while `plays` is null), `thinking` whether a search runs, `error` `null` or `{kind, detail}` (`kind` in snake_case: `launch_failed`, `timeout`, `process_died`, `protocol_error`, `illegal_move`, `no_move`, `bad_request`, `cancelled`, `unsupported`; `detail` the sentence of the log). A pick of Stockfish succeeds whenever a Stockfish file is found, so a launch that fails shows only at the first search, as `error` `launch_failed`; a pick of `own` always succeeds |
| `list_actions` | `{"type": "move", "uci": "e2e4"}` for every legal move (a promotion is four); empty once the game is over |
| `perform_action` | plays one of them and answers `{move, status, turn, winner, inCheck}`; errors `illegal_action`, `promotion_required` (`e7e8` without the piece), `game_over`, `invalid_params` (not a UCI move) |
| `reset` | the opening position; takes no options; **keeps the opponent** (side, level and engine; a standing error goes by the version rule) |
| `set_state` | `{"fen": ".."}` and/or `{"opponent": {"plays": "white"\|"black"\|null, "level": "easy"\|"medium"\|"hard"\|"max", "engine": "stockfish"\|"own"}}` (inside `opponent` a missing member means unchanged; `{}` changes nothing). A FEN starts a game from any position `FromFen` accepts, finished ones included (the pieces in the scene are dealt out again, `SetPosition`). Neither of the two, a side that is not `white` / `black` / null, an unknown level, an engine that is not exactly `stockfish` or `own` (`"Own"`, `""`, null, a number), a member of the wrong type: `invalid_params`, and nothing is changed (everything is checked first). The opponent is applied first, then the position. No engine for the pick (no Stockfish found; only Stockfish can fail): `engine_unavailable` with the engine's sentence; the pick is **refused as a whole**: the position is **not** replaced and the opponent is **unchanged** (side, level, engine and a running search stay as they were, also when the program played before: a client that asks for stockfish while `own` plays as black gets `engine_unavailable` and `own` still plays black; the level of a refused pick is not saved either, so `{"opponent": {"engine": "own", "plays": "black"}}` is the way on from two people) and `opponent.error` has the reason (until the version moves by someone else or the next pick; before this change the program played nobody after a refusal). A pick that changes nothing does nothing (no restart, no new version); `plays: null` clears an error |

- The state version is `ChessGameModel::GetVersion()` plus `OpponentController::GetRevision()`. It grows on a played move, a reset, a `set_state`, a pick of the side, level or engine and an error that appears or goes, not on a selection or on a search that starts or ends (`thinking`). A person's move by mouse and the program's move grow it too, so `state_changed` reports them. It never goes back.
- `list_actions` is unchanged by the opponent: every action is a move, which `tools/stockfish_bridge.py` relies on. `perform_action` is not blocked for the program's side: a controller may play for it (a search that this makes stale is dropped by the version); only the mouse is kept from the program's side.
- The scene follows the model: a move, a promotion, a reset and a `set_state` all show in a `screenshot`, which is how a headless run is checked by eye.

## Rules

Standard chess, Chess960 excluded: legal moves only (pins, check, castling, en passant, promotion), checkmate, stalemate and the draws that end the game by themselves: insufficient material, threefold repetition and 50 moves without a capture or a pawn move. FIDE makes a player claim the last two (it ends a game by itself only at fivefold and 75 moves), but the window has no way to claim, so they apply automatically. Both thresholds are named constants in `GameStatus.cpp`.

- A move is a `Move` from `MoveGenerator::GenerateLegal()`; `ChessGameModel::TryMove()` accepts nothing else and answers `std::expected<void, MoveError>` (`GameOver`, `IllegalMove`). `Position::IsLegal()` assumes a pseudo-legal move from the generator (while in check it relies on the generator having restricted the targets), so do not call it with a hand-made move: parse text with `MoveGenerator::ParseUci()`.
- Castling is the king's move (`e1g1`), as in UCI. A promoting pawn has four moves per target, queen first; a click on the target leaves the promotion pending in the model (`ChessGameModel::HasPendingPromotion()`) and `ChoosePromotion(piece)` plays it (the window's modal `PromotionPanel` makes that call, see "The promotion picker"); `TryMove` / UCI (`e7e8n`) name the piece themselves, as the program and the control API do.
- `Position` keeps the en passant square only when a pawn can capture onto it (so positions that are equal for the rules have equal keys and FEN). `FromFen` rejects positions the rules cannot reach in the ways that would break the lookups: a king count other than one, a pawn on a back rank, more than 16 pieces of one color (the scene has 16 piece nodes per color, so any accepted position can be shown), the side that just moved in check, castling rights without the king and rook in place.
- A finished game accepts no move. The window shows the end with the red king (bright on check, dark on mate), the dimmed board and a bar across it (white, black or gray for a draw); the HUD says who won and why ("White wins", "Checkmate"), and the reason also goes to the log. A click on the board after the end, or the Reset button, starts a new game.
- The magic bitboards are searched for on first use (about 90 ms in a Debug build, measured on macOS) with a fixed seed, every candidate checked against the slow ray walk. If that becomes too slow, print the found numbers once and embed them as constants.

New rule code goes into `Game/` with a gtest case; do not patch around the generator in the model or the components.

`tests/control_smoke.py` is not a gtest: ctest runs it as `BagiChessControlSmoke` (`be_add_game_control_tests`). It starts `BagiChess --control --headless`, once on the real clock and once with `--manual-clock`, plays four moves, refusals, a mate in one loaded with `set_state`, a promotion, and checks that screenshots follow the model. A third run plays four moves before the first frame (the manual clock has none until `step`) and checks that the scene equals the one set from the same FEN. **The own-engine scenario** runs on both clocks and needs nothing from the environment (no process, no Stockfish, no wrapper, so it also runs where the Stockfish scenario is left out): the default `opponent.engine` is `stockfish`; `set_state {"opponent": {"engine": "own", "plays": "black", "level": "easy"}}` reads back with the version grown and no error; `perform_action e2e4`; wait until two moves are played and nothing thinks (a time limit of 30 s, no fixed sleep); the reply is a legal Black move and the game is ongoing; after a `reset` (which keeps the engine) the same `e2e4` gets **the same reply** (a fixed depth is deterministic, which is the point of the own engine on the manual clock); then `set_state {"opponent": {"engine": "stockfish", "plays": null}}` and two people play by hand, each move taken from `list_actions` (not typed, so the scenario does not depend on the reply the engine gave), the program staying out over ten frames. **The opponent-panel scenario** runs on both clocks too and needs nothing from the environment (it picks the own engine): it drives the game with `ui_click` on elements found by name and reads them back with `ui_tree`, as a person would (the manual clock `step`s between a click and a read). `ui_tree` shows the `OpponentPanel` canvas hidden and modal and the `Opponent` button saying "Two players" with the action `ChessOpenOpponent`; `ui_click Opponent` opens the panel, whose segments `ModeTwo`, `ColorWhite`, `EngineStockfish` and `LevelMedium` are `selected`, and no group disabled, the error line empty; `LevelHard` and `ColorBlack` clicked with two people playing are read back (`level` "hard", `plays` null) and `ColorWhite` puts the color back; `EngineOwn` and `ModeComputer` are read back from `get_state` (`opponent.plays` "black", `engine` "own", no error, the groups enabled, the button says "You: White"), then `LevelEasy` and `ColorWhite`; `tests/fake_uci_engine.py` is the stand-in for Stockfish of the opponent tests (it is not `tools/tests/fake_uci_engine.py`, the pytest one of the bridge): Python 3, standard library only, written from the UCI protocol alone. It answers `uci`, `isready` and `go` with scripted moves (`--moves e2e4,e7e5,...`, then `bestmove (none)`) and can think for a while and obey `stop` (`--think-ms`, `--think-first-only`), hang, die (`--die-on-go`, `--die-once MARKER`), stay silent after `uci`, write lines in two pieces, flood or garble its output, and refuse to leave on `quit`, end of input or `SIGTERM`; `--log FILE` records every line it receives. The flags are listed in the docstring of the script. The C++ side is `FakeEngineUtil.h` (`FakeEngineLaunch({flags})`, `TempDirectory` named after the suite and the test, `ReadFileLines`, `Lines`, `WaitForLogLine` to poll the log for a line, `SKIP_WITHOUT_FAKE_ENGINE()`); the root `CMakeLists.txt` gives `ChessTests` the interpreter as `CHESS_TEST_PYTHON` (the Python of the engine's CMake, the venv one when there is one), and a test that starts the fake skips itself when that is empty or on Windows. The process code is POSIX only (`UciProcess::Start` answers `Unsupported` on Windows, where `UciProcessTest` has the one test that checks it); a Windows implementation is a later step.

`ChessTestUtil.h` has the helpers (`Sq`, `Mv`, `PositionFromFen`, the perft FENs). `SeededRandom.h` (header only) is the xorshift64 generator with a fixed seed that `OwnEngineTest`, `StockfishRefereeTest` and (through `GamePlay.h`) `StockfishGameTest` use (the same numbers on every platform, unlike `std::uniform_int_distribution`). `StockfishUtil.h` (header only) is how a test finds the real Stockfish: `StockfishPath()` (`FindStockfish({})`, nothing on Windows), `StockfishSettings(path)` for a `UciEngine`, `Describe(error)` (an `EngineError` as a sentence) and `SKIP_WITHOUT_STOCKFISH(path)`, which declares `path` or skips the test (the tests ship and install nothing of Stockfish). `GamePlay.h` (header only) is how a test plays a whole game through a `ChessGameModel`: `Player` (the next move for the side to move), `EnginePlayer(engine, limits)` (any `IChessEngine`, through `SearchRequest::FromModel` so the history goes with the request; an error fails the test), `RandomPlayer(random)` and `PlayGame(model, white, black, plyCap)` (every move must be accepted; `kDefaultPlyCap` = 120), used by `OwnEngineTest` and `StockfishGameTest`. `HudWords.h` (header only) has `AllHudWords()`: every word the HUD can show, taken from `StatusText` over a scratch game and from `OpponentText` (`turn`, `status`, `side`, `opponentError`: "" and every `EngineErrorKind` sentence), and `Rounds()` and `Publish(round)`, which sets those four `UIVars` to the word `round` of each list; the layout tests of the HUD and the panel (`ChessUiTest`, `ChessHudWebTest`) walk the rounds. The names of the segments of the panel (modes, colors, engines, levels) are not in it: they are elements of the scene that the tests walk and hold to the tables. `TestEngines.h` has the engine doubles that the tests of what uses an `IChessEngine` share (header only): `InstantEngine` (the first legal move), `MatingEngine` (a mate in one when there is one, else the first legal move: it ends a game with the program's move), `GatedEngine` (holds the answer until `Open()`, honors its token or ignores it, optionally a move chosen in advance), `FailingEngine`, all derived from `RecordingEngine` (`Calls()`, `Answered()`, `LastLimits()`), `CountingFactory` (an `EngineFactory` that logs its calls, the level ids and the engine ids (`Engines()`) and hands out engines in order, or fails), and `WaitUntil` (polls with a time limit, no fixed sleep). `ChessUiTest` needs `ChessModule` linked whole (`WHOLE_ARCHIVE` in `CMakeLists.txt`), since the scene file names components that no test references. The rest of the scene glue (the result bar, the promotion sprite, how the highlights look) draws and is not covered by tests: check it by hand in `BagiChess`.
